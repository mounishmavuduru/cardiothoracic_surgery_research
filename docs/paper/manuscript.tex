\date{July 2026 (working preprint)}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We ask whether a closed-form spectral graph biomarker predicts reentry
inducibility in atrial tissue beyond established fibrosis and connectivity
features. The biomarker is the \textbf{Spectral Fragility Index (SFI)}, the
Fiedler-value sensitivity $\partial\lam_2/\partial w_{ij}=(\phii_i-\phii_j)^2$
aggregated under a fibrosis-weighted conduction-uncoupling field. Pre-registered
before any labels were seen, the primary endpoint was $\dAUC\geq 0.05$ with a
DeLong paired $p<0.05$ on shape-family-held-out folds. \emph{The endpoint is not
met, but the strength of the negative differs by regime, and we separate them.}
On up to $100{,}000$ synthetic excitable networks SFI adds no practically
meaningful predictive value (subspace $\dAUC\approx+0.003$; $\sim\!4{,}150$ cases
needed at $80\%$ power, resolvable only because $N$ is huge, and so
\emph{clinically undetectable} there). On two real cohorts ($182$ patients:
Roney $n=100$, UW/Boyle $n=82$) the combined gradient-boosting estimate is
$+0.012$ with a bootstrap interval $[-0.017,+0.044]$ that excludes the
threshold, so the real-anatomy result is a measured null rather than a
shortfall of power. An earlier version of this work reported $+0.051$ here and
called the real-cohort evidence inconclusive. Correcting a defect in one
cohort's released substrate, which we found only afterwards, moved that figure
to $+0.036$ at the original cohort size, and extending the Roney arm from $62$
to all $100$ released meshes moved it to $+0.012$, so most of the drop was
sample size rather than substrate. The audit that found the defect, and the four
controls that followed, are reported in full; the cause of that cohort's
unusually low inducibility rate turned out not to be any substrate defect but a
$30\%$ difference in fibrosis burden between the two released cohorts, which
accounts for the apparent anomaly and reproduces it in either direction when the
burden is swapped. Where the
null is clean, only half the mechanism is feature redundancy: the aggregate SFI
summaries are reconstructible from the competitor set at $R^2=0.970$, while the extreme
and edge-level columns retain variance of their own ($R^2<0.25$) and still do
not predict the label. The exact per-region $\Delta\lam_2$ SFI, which has no
first-order approximation error, also adds nothing over the competitor set. A separate,
label-free result explains why the \emph{single-vector} SFI is also
ill-conditioned on real tissue. It is valid only while
$\rhoval=\lVert\Delta L\rVert/(\lam_3-\lam_2)=O(1)$, and real atria have
median $\rhoval\approx 2422$, at least $272\times$ past the empirical
$\rhoval^\ast\approx 3$ on every mesh tested, so by Davis--Kahan the Fiedler
vector is unstable. We do \emph{not} claim $\rhoval$ proves non-predictiveness;
it bounds the
accuracy of the $\Delta\lam_2$ \emph{magnitude}, not the classification content
of a ranking feature. The spectral gap collapses as $N^{-1.05}$ on the real
atrial surface (the 2-manifold Weyl exponent), so $\rhoval$ grows lawfully with
resolution. One component partially survives: the Fiedler-gradient localizer
$\lvert\nabla\phii_2\rvert$ is a weak but real rank localizer (origin in the top
$\sim\!18\%$ on synthetic networks, and weaker on real anatomy, where only
$\sim\!20$ origins are available) that beats graph-centrality baselines and
transfers across three dynamical media, though it co-localizes with substrate
imaging rather than beating it, and substrate imaging is the better localizer in
the FHN arm ($\sim\!0.10$ against $0.176$), for a reason we make precise. Naive
methodology (random CV,
$p$-value thresholds, no effect-size gate) would have reported SFI as working;
our pre-registered protocol rejects it. Our own convergence study surfaces a key
limitation: the inducibility labeller is strongly resolution-sensitive and, on a
completed six-tier study ($1500$--$48000$ nodes, $n=24$), does not converge
within the tested window; the direction of the residual bias at the
$2{,}000$-node operating resolution is not established, since the finest tier
($37.5\%$) sits above the operating-resolution rate ($34.0\%$). Our contribution is a carefully
bounded negative result: the mechanism behind it, a label-free criterion for the
estimator's validity, a characterization of how it scales, and a demonstration of
the false positives that standard methodology produces.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%======================================================================
Algebraic connectivity $\lam_2$ (Fiedler 1973~\cite{fiedler1973}) governs the
stability of coupled excitable and oscillator networks (the
master-stability-function result~\cite{pecora1998} and the synchronizability
eigenratio $\lam_N/\lam_2$~\cite{barahona2002}), and has been used as a
real-time seizure detector~\cite{bomela2020}. The per-edge sensitivity
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\lam_2}{\partial w_{ij}}=(\phii_{2,i}-\phii_{2,j})^2
\label{eq:edge}
\end{equation}
is a classical identity underlying Fiedler-vector edge-editing
heuristics~\cite{ghosh2006}. It is therefore natural to hypothesize that
aggregating this sensitivity over a fibrosis-weighted perioperative uncoupling
field yields a per-region biomarker of atrial-fibrillation (AF) reentry
fragility. We test this hypothesis and \emph{pre-register the null}
(``SFI merely re-encodes fibrosis'') as an accepted, reportable outcome,
so that a negative result is a success of the protocol rather than a
file-drawer casualty.

The mathematics we use is classical; we claim no new theorem. Our contributions are
(i)~a leakage-controlled incremental test of SFI against real competitors and a
spatial null on real anatomy; (ii)~a label-free validity criterion and a
measured gap-scaling exponent that
matches the Weyl 2-manifold prediction on real atria, which together explain why
the \emph{single-vector estimator} is ill-conditioned on real tissue and why any
first-order Fiedler-based marker faces the same conditioning problem there, and
which do not explain the predictive null; (iii)~a cross-medium transfer analysis
isolating the one
component that generalizes; and (iv)~a falsification-protocol demonstration
showing that standard methodology would have manufactured exactly the false
positive this protocol catches.

In brief, a closed-form spectral
fragility index does \emph{not} add practically meaningful reentry-inducibility
prediction beyond existing features, either at synthetic scale or on $182$
patients of real anatomy. The mechanism is partly feature redundancy and partly a
feature that is genuinely new but unpredictive (Section~\ref{sec:redundancy}).
Independently, the
single-vector estimator is provably ill-conditioned on real tissue
($\rhoval\gg 1$). What we report is not a discovery but a carefully drawn
boundary: where a Fiedler-based estimator is numerically valid, why real tissue
lies far outside it, and how readily naive methodology mistakes redundant,
inconclusive signal for a working biomarker.

\section{Theory: the Spectral Fragility Index}
\label{sec:theory}
%======================================================================
The core mathematics is classical numerical linear algebra; the
contribution is its \emph{use}. We state each result with its prior art
(Section~\ref{sec:credits}) and make the validity boundary explicit
(Section~\ref{sec:validity}).

\subsection{The weighted conduction graph}
Nodes are mesh vertices; edges are triangle edges; the edge weight $w_{ij}>0$
is a fibre-anisotropic conduction conductance attenuated by local fibrosis. The
combinatorial Laplacian is
\begin{equation}
L=D-W,\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}\Big(\textstyle\sum_j w_{ij}\Big).
\end{equation}
$L$ is symmetric positive-semidefinite with a single zero eigenvalue (the
constant vector) for a connected graph, with spectrum
$0=\lam_1<\lam_2\leq\lam_3\leq\cdots$ and eigenvectors $\phii_1,\phii_2,\dots$;
$\lam_2$ is the algebraic connectivity and $\phii_2$ the Fiedler vector.

\subsection{The closed-form fragility (first order)}
$L$ is a sum of rank-one edge terms, so
\begin{equation}
L=\sum_{(i,j)\in E} w_{ij}\,(e_i-e_j)(e_i-e_j)^{\!\top}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\frac{\partial L}{\partial w_{ij}}=(e_i-e_j)(e_i-e_j)^{\!\top}.
\label{eq:dL}
\end{equation}
For a \emph{simple} eigenvalue $\lam$ with unit eigenvector $\phii$,
first-order (Hellmann--Feynman) perturbation theory gives
$\partial\lam/\partial w_{ij}=\phii^{\!\top}(\partial L/\partial w_{ij})\phii
=(\phii_i-\phii_j)^2$, which for the Fiedler pair is Eq.~\eqref{eq:edge}: a
non-negative per-edge \emph{fragility}. Reducing a conductance $w_{ij}$ by a
small $\Delta w_{ij}\geq 0$ (uncoupling) lowers $\lam_2$ by
$\Delta w_{ij}(\phii_{2,i}-\phii_{2,j})^2$ to first order.

The identity is verified in six independent ways rather than merely asserted.
Computer algebra: (i)~sympy proves Eqs.~\eqref{eq:edge}--\eqref{eq:dL}
symbolically, and (ii)~on a concrete $5$-node graph the finite-difference
$\partial\lam_2/\partial w_{ij}$ matches $(\phii_{2,i}-\phii_{2,j})^2$ to
$\lvert\text{err}\rvert\approx 2\times10^{-17}$ (mpmath, $40$ digits). Exactness:
(iii)~the identity holds \emph{exactly} with rational weights (zero floating
point); (iv)~an interval-arithmetic enclosure certifies the true $\lam_2$ to
within $\sim\!4\times10^{-40}$; and (v)~$\lam_2/\phii_2$ agree across scipy,
networkx, and a
hand-rolled inverse iteration (spread $2.5\times10^{-15}$). These three
tolerances are printed at run time by \code{scripts/verify\_sfi\_identity.py} and
\code{scripts/verify\_math\_rigor.py} rather than stored in \code{results/}, so
they are reproduced by rerunning those scripts. Finally, (vi)~the
algebraic core is kernel-checked in the Coq proof assistant over the
exact rationals, axiom-free (Appendix~\ref{app:formal}). The analytic
Hellmann--Feynman step (differentiability of a simple eigenvalue) is the cited
classical result~\cite{ghosh2006}, not re-proved here.

\subsection{Per-region aggregation: the SFI}
Model diffuse perioperative stress as a distribution of small, non-negative,
fibrosis-weighted fractional conductance reductions $\Delta w$ (mean
$\mathbb{E}[\Delta w_{ij}]=\mathrm{frac}_{ij}\,w_{ij}$, calibrated to literature
perioperative conduction-velocity slowing; $\Delta w\approx 0.36$, frozen in the
pre-registration). The per-region Spectral Fragility Index is the expected
$\lam_2$ drop contributed by region $R$:
\begin{equation}
\SFI(R)=\mathbb{E}[\Delta\lam_2\text{ from }R]
\approx\underbrace{\sum_{(i,j)\in R}\mathbb{E}[\Delta w_{ij}]\,(\phii_{2,i}-\phii_{2,j})^2}_{\text{first order}}
+\underbrace{\sum_{k\neq2}\frac{(\phii_k^{\!\top}\Delta
L\,\phii_2)^2}{\lam_2-\lam_k}}_{\text{2nd-order resolvent}}
+O(\lVert\Delta L\rVert^3).
\label{eq:sfi}
\end{equation}
The second-order term is the source of the validity radius below. Near
eigenvalue degeneracy ($\lam_2\approx\lam_3$) the single $\phii_2$ is an
arbitrary rotation in the low invariant subspace, so we use a basis-independent
\emph{subspace (projector) SFI}: with $P=\sum_c\phii_c\phii_c^{\!\top}$ over the
clustered subspace, $s_{ij}^{\!\top}Ps_{ij}=\sum_c(\phii_{c,i}-\phii_{c,j})^2$
($s_{ij}=e_i-e_j$) is well-defined. For discrete edits (Maze lesion,
atriotomy) the expansion is abandoned and $\lam_2$ is recomputed \emph{exactly}
after the rank-one edit.

\subsection{The validity radius (the central caveat)}
\label{sec:validity}
The first-order form is trustworthy only while the perturbation is small
\emph{relative to the spectral gap} $\lam_3-\lam_2$. Two different tools bound
two different things (a common error is to use Weyl for both):
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item Weyl's inequality bounds the \emph{eigenvalue} move:
      $\lvert\Delta\lam_2\rvert\leq\lVert\Delta L\rVert$.
\item Davis--Kahan~\cite{daviskahan1970} bounds the
      \emph{eigenvector} move, which scales like
      $\lVert\Delta L\rVert/(\lam_3-\lam_2)$. This, not Weyl, governs when
      $\phii_2$ (hence the single-vector SFI) degrades.
\end{itemize}
Define the \emph{validity radius}
\begin{equation}
\rhoval:=\frac{\lVert\Delta L\rVert}{\lam_3-\lam_2}.
\end{equation}
From second-order Rayleigh--Schr\"odinger theory the relative error of the
first-order $\Delta\lam_2$ prediction is $\Theta(\rhoval)$, and the Fiedler
vector rotates by $\sin\angle(\phii_2,\phii_2')\leq\sqrt{2}\,\rhoval$
(Davis--Kahan). Empirically the error is $1\%$ at $\rhoval=0.3$, crosses $10\%$
at $\rhoval^\ast\approx 3$, and reaches $92\%$ at $\rhoval=28$, with Weyl holding
throughout. On real excitable media the gap is tiny, so $\rhoval$ is
enormous. Over the $12$ scanned meshes the median is $\rhoval\approx2422$ with an
interquartile range of $[1082,\,7384]$ and a full range of $[817,\,16554]$; the
spread is wide because $\rhoval$ inherits the near-degenerate gap in its
denominator, but every mesh exceeds $\rhoval^\ast$ and even the smallest does so
by a factor of $272$, so the conclusion does not rest on the median. That is about
$10^3\times$ past
$\rhoval^\ast$.

The criterion $\rhoval$ proves that the
\emph{single-vector} first-order SFI is a numerically \emph{invalid estimator of
$\Delta\lam_2$} on real tissue, and that $\phii_2$ is ill-conditioned
(Davis--Kahan), a label-free statement about the estimator. It does
\emph{not} prove SFI is non-predictive: $\rhoval$ bounds the accuracy of the
$\Delta\lam_2$ \emph{magnitude}, not the classification content of a monotone
ranking feature; indeed the \emph{exact} $\Delta\lam_2$ SFI (no $\rhoval$
limitation) is \emph{also} non-predictive (Section~\ref{sec:null}), so the
predictive null has a separate cause, which Section~\ref{sec:redundancy} measures
and finds to be only half redundancy. Two caveats:
(i)~$\rhoval$'s numerator carries the fibrosis-weighted $\Delta w$ field, so only
its denominator $\lam_3-\lam_2$ is truly label-free; (ii)~$\rhoval^\ast\approx3$
is calibrated on a $16$-node path graph with a problem-dependent constant, so
``$10^3\times$ past $\rhoval^\ast$'' is order-of-magnitude, not exact.

\subsection{Prior art (this is not new mathematics)}
\label{sec:credits}
The identity~\eqref{eq:edge} is Ghosh \& Boyd (2006)~\cite{ghosh2006}; algebraic
connectivity is Fiedler (1973)~\cite{fiedler1973}; $\lam_2$ governing coupled
excitable/oscillator stability is Pecora \& Carroll
(1998)~\cite{pecora1998} with the eigenratio of Barahona \& Pecora
(2002)~\cite{barahona2002}; Weyl's law, Davis--Kahan, and second-order
perturbation theory are classical (1970s and earlier). No result in
Section~\ref{sec:theory} is a new theorem, and none is presented as one. The
contribution is recognizing that $\rhoval$ is the right \emph{a priori}
validity screen for a spectral fragility biomarker, measuring its violation on
real tissue, and using it to \emph{mechanistically explain} a negative result.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
%======================================================================
Full detail is in the pre-registration (\code{docs/\allowbreak
PRE\_REGISTRATION.md}) and
theory note (\code{docs/\allowbreak SFI\_THEORY.md}); the endpoints,
cross-validation scheme,
baseline set, and accepted null were frozen before any labels were inspected.

\paragraph{The edge weight.} Everything downstream --- $\lam_2$, the Fiedler
vector, the spectral gap, $\rhoval$ and the SFI itself --- is a function of the
conduction graph's edge weights, so we give their form explicitly. For an edge
$(i,j)$ let $\hat{e}_{ij}$ be the unit vector along it, $\bar{f}_{ij}$ the
normalised mean of the two endpoint fibre directions, and
$a_{ij}=\lvert\hat{e}_{ij}\cdot\bar{f}_{ij}\rvert$ their absolute alignment. With
$\phi_{ij}=\tfrac12(\phi_i+\phi_j)$ the mean endpoint fibrosis,
\begin{equation}
w_{ij}=\underbrace{\bigl[c_\perp+(c_\parallel-c_\perp)\,a_{ij}^{2}\bigr]}_{\text{anisotropy}}
\cdot\underbrace{\mathrm{clip}\bigl(1-\phi_{ij},\,\varepsilon,\,1\bigr)}_{\text{fibrosis}},
\label{eq:weight}
\end{equation}
with $c_\parallel=1.0$ along fibre, $c_\perp=0.3$ across, and a floor
$\varepsilon=0.05$ so dense scar retains a small residual conductance rather than
disconnecting the graph. The $a^{2}$ dependence makes the weight a function of
alignment rather than of direction, so it is invariant to fibre sign, which is
the only property of $\bar{f}$ that is identifiable. Note that both factors are
bounded, so $w_{ij}\in[\varepsilon c_\perp,\,c_\parallel]$; no edge weight can
vanish and the graph stays connected by construction.

\paragraph{Cohort flow.} The Roney deposit contains $100$ meshes and all $100$
are used. The UW deposit contains $164$ meshes, which are $82$ patients imaged
twice, before and after ablation; the primary analysis uses the $82$
\emph{pre-ablation} meshes only, since post-ablation geometry encodes the
delivered treatment. No subject is excluded for any other reason, so the analysed
cohort is $100+82=182$ and there is no attrition to report. The $164$-mesh figure
appears in this paper only where a statement concerns the released files
themselves, such as the fibre-field and element-tag audits.

\paragraph{Inducibility protocol.} Labels come from a monodomain
Mitchell--Schaeffer solve on the coarsened surface ($2000$ nodes, vertex
clustering). Membrane parameters, frozen before any label was generated:
$\tau_{\mathrm{in}}=0.3$, $\tau_{\mathrm{out}}=6.0$, $\tau_{\mathrm{open}}=120$,
$\tau_{\mathrm{close}}=110$~ms and $v_{\mathrm{gate}}=0.13$, with fibrosis
shortening $\tau_{\mathrm{close}}$ by up to $50\%$. Diffusion uses an anisotropic
conductance-weighted cotangent Laplacian with $d_0=0.20$ and $w_{\mathrm{ref}}=0.3$,
integrated by backward Euler at $\Delta t=0.05$~ms. Induction is burst pacing from
$2$ randomly chosen sites, $6$ beats at a cycle length of $150$~ms, stimulus
amplitude $0.15$ over $2$~ms within a $4$~mm radius. A subject is labelled
inducible if self-sustained supra-threshold activity persists at least $650$~ms
after the last stimulus, observed over a $1000$~ms window. The protocol was
verified before use: APD90 $218$~ms healthy and $121$~ms fibrotic, planar
conduction velocity $0.4$--$1.2$~m/s, and a monotone relationship between fibrosis
and sustained reentry.

\paragraph{Evaluation pipeline.} Cross-validation is \code{GroupKFold} with the
group set to the patient, so no subject appears in both training and test folds,
using $k=\min(5,\,n_{\mathrm{groups}})$ folds. Two classifiers are run. Logistic
regression is a standardisation-plus-\code{LogisticRegression} pipeline
(\code{liblinear}, at most $2000$ iterations) whose regularisation $C$ is chosen
in an \emph{inner} \code{GroupKFold} over $\{0.03,0.1,0.3,1.0,3.0\}$, so tuning
never sees the outer test fold. Gradient boosting is deliberately \emph{not}
tuned --- $120$ trees, depth $2$, learning rate $0.05$, subsample $0.8$ --- because
at these sample sizes a search would fit the folds; this is a fixed, shallow
reference rather than a competitive model. We log it as a deviation from the
pre-registered nested selection, which specifies an inner \code{GroupKFold}
search for both classifiers. Out-of-fold predicted probabilities
are pooled across folds and compared by the DeLong test. Pooling out-of-fold
scores means the two compared vectors come from $k$ different fitted models
rather than from two fixed scoring functions, which is DeLong's assumption; the
paired structure and the leakage-free grouping are preserved, but the resulting
$p$-values should be read as approximate. The naive comparison reported alongside
replaces the grouping with shuffled \code{KFold}. On the real cohorts each
patient contributes one row and is its own group, so \code{GroupKFold} there
coincides with unshuffled \code{KFold} and the grouping is a guarantee rather
than a correction; the leakage it exists to prevent is demonstrated on the
replicated synthetic cohort, where resamples of a single shape family have
several members (Section~\ref{sec:falsify}).

\paragraph{Substrate.} Real left-atrial surfaces are loaded into weighted
conduction graphs from two public cohorts: the Roney LA virtual cohort (Zenodo
5801337~\cite{roney2022}) and the UW/Boyle LGE-MRI cohort (Dryad
\code{10.5061/dryad.kkwh70sg0}, $82$ distinct AF patients~\cite{uwboyle2025}).
Each mesh carries an image-intensity-ratio (IIR) fibrosis proxy mapped to a
fibrosis field by the Khurram healthy-to-scar ramp~\cite{khurram2014}. The two
cohorts differ in what else they carry: the Roney meshes ship registered
universal atrial coordinates and per-vertex fibre fields (directional spread
$0.945$--$0.979$), whereas the UW meshes ship a \code{VECTORS fiber} array that
is the constant $(1,0,0)$ everywhere (spread $0.000$) and no atrial coordinates,
for which we substitute a PCA chart, so UW anisotropy is a fixed coordinate bias
rather than anatomy (Sections~\ref{sec:regions} and~\ref{sec:substrate}). Meshes
are coarsened to
$\sim\!2000$-node graphs by field-preserving vertex clustering.

\paragraph{Labels.} Ground truth is a monodomain Mitchell--Schaeffer
reaction--diffusion inducibility verdict~\cite{mitchell2003} (self-sustained
reentry $\geq 650$~ms after burst pacing at cycle length $150$~ms from random
sites), run on each real anatomy with an anisotropic conductance-weighted
cotangent Laplacian and implicit time stepping. Reentry here is governed by
wavelength (CV$\times$ERP), source--sink mismatch, and unidirectional block,
\emph{never} by $\lam_2$, so a predictive SFI$\to$label link is not circular.
These are monodomain simulator verdicts, not clinical post-operative AF.
openCARP is run alongside on all $182$ subjects as a robustness check rather
than as the labeller (\code{results/\allowbreak opencarp\_full\_cohort.json}),
and agrees with the monodomain verdict on $156/182$.

\paragraph{The SFI feature and its competitors.} Per-region SFI is computed as
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:sfi} (first-order, subspace, and exact-recompute variants). It
is evaluated strictly as an add-on/ablation against a non-trivial competitor set
that it must beat: fibrosis burden, fibrosis spatial entropy, fibrosis patch
size, deterministic global min-cut (Stoer--Wagner), percolation threshold, and
$\lam_2$-alone (isolating what the \emph{SFI construction} adds over the bare
algebraic connectivity).

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol (the guards).} Pre-registered: shape-family
\code{GroupKFold} (each patient is its own group, so cross-validation is
patient-held-out; grouped \emph{and} naive AUC both reported so leakage is
visible), the DeLong paired test~\cite{delong1988} in its fast midrank
form~\cite{sunxu2014}, $\geq 10^4$ stratified bootstrap resamples (a logged
deviation: the Kuramoto transfer arm was run at $5\times10^3$), a
rotational/torus-shift spatial null for localization, tie-robust permutation
nulls, and a keep/delete rule deciding each sub-claim by \emph{measured}
correlation. Effect sizes are gated, not just $p$-values. The primary endpoint
requires \emph{both} grouped $\dAUC\geq 0.05$ \emph{and} DeLong $p<0.05$.

\paragraph{Synthetic media, generative model.} The synthetic cohorts are random
geometric graphs, which give a two-dimensional excitable sheet with the
degree heterogeneity of real tissue and none of its anatomy. Each network draws
$n$ points uniformly on the unit square and connects every pair within a fixed
radius; if that radius leaves the graph too sparse the construction falls back to
a symmetric $6$-nearest-neighbour graph, and in either case only the largest
connected component is retained, so the medium is a single sheet rather than
several. Lesion burden is swept uniformly over $[0.05,\,0.45]$ across the cohort,
which is what makes both classes appear: at the low end the network is stable and
at the high end it is not, mirroring the mild-to-severe fibrosis gradient of the
atrial cohorts. Because the topology is generated rather than measured, these
cohorts carry no fibre field and no anatomical coordinates, and they are used
only where those are irrelevant --- for the scale behaviour of the null and for
the cross-medium transfer of the localizer.

\paragraph{Transfer media.} The identical calculus is applied to
FitzHugh--Nagumo (FHN) excitable networks and Kuramoto phase-oscillator networks
(a different dynamical class), where $\lam_2$ governs synchronizability, to
separate what is a property of the mathematics from an accident of one dataset.

The negative is not uniform, and we separate the regimes. On the
\emph{synthetic} cohorts (up to $100{,}000$ networks) the null is clean: the
effect converges to $\dAUC\approx+0.003$, statistically resolvable only because
$N$ is enormous. On the \emph{real} cohorts it is a measured null.
The combined gradient-boosting point estimate is $+0.012$ (DeLong $p=0.449$,
bootstrap CI $[-0.017,+0.044]$), roughly a quarter of the pre-registered
$0.05$ threshold and with an interval that excludes it. An earlier version of
this analysis reported $+0.051$ here and had to argue that the endpoint was
missed only on significance. That estimate was an artefact of the sealed UW
substrate: correcting it drops the figure to $+0.036$ at the original cohort
size and to $+0.012$ once the Roney arm is extended to all $100$ released
meshes. With $42$ inducible of $182$ this is the largest real-anatomy arm in
the study, and the null here is a measurement rather than a shortfall. The
combined row is a single pooled ROC across two cohorts of unequal prevalence
($34/100$ on Roney, $8/82$ on UW), so its ranking carries between-cohort as well
as within-cohort separability; that is why the pooled base AUC under logistic
regression, $0.884$, exceeds both components ($0.870$ and $0.834$), and the
pooled increment should be read alongside the cohort-specific estimates rather
than in place of them.

The UW cohort remains the weak arm, though not for the reason we previously
gave. On eight positives its intervals are wide, the competitor comparison
spanning $[-0.095,+0.203]$ under gradient boosting, so it is an underpowered
external check of the \emph{labeller} rather than of SFI. We had also described
its competitor baseline as close to chance, and that was simply wrong: the
measured competitor base AUC is $0.834$ under logistic regression and $0.748$
under gradient boosting. Only the \emph{fibrosis-only} baseline approaches
chance, at $0.562$, and it is that near-chance baseline which manufactures the
cohort's largest apparent effect, $\dAUC=+0.217$ with $p=0.121$ and an interval
$[-0.030,+0.485]$ that spans half the available range. A large ratio over a
baseline that predicts almost nothing, on eight events, is not a signal.

The null is not explained by the validity radius. The
\emph{exact} per-region $\Delta\lam_2$ SFI, which carries no first-order
approximation error and is immune to the $\rhoval$ argument, also adds nothing
over the competitor set. Our reading is that SFI's information is already
present in standard connectivity features ($\lam_2$-alone, min-cut,
percolation), but an incremental null does not by itself separate that
redundancy from a feature that carries little predictive information at all. We
separate the two in Section~\ref{sec:redundancy}, directly and without reference
to the label, where the redundancy account holds for the region-level aggregates
and fails for the extreme and edge-level columns. Either way the incremental null
is itself a label-dependent finding, separate from $\rhoval$.

\paragraph{Fibrosis-heterogeneity baseline comparison (pre-specified as an
additional feature-set contrast), and its withdrawal.} This is not the
pre-registered secondary endpoint, which is the localization test reported in
Section~\ref{sec:localize}; it was pre-specified as a further feature-set
comparison alongside the primary one. An earlier
version of this analysis reported that SFI beat a
\emph{fibrosis-heterogeneity-only} baseline on the combined real cohort (GBT
$\dAUC=+0.103$, $p=0.009$) and read that as evidence SFI is not merely
re-encoded fibrosis but carries connectivity information beyond the substrate.
That result does not survive, and we withdraw both it and the claim it
supported. Two changes separate the old number from the new one, and they do
not contribute equally; both figures below are gradient-boosting $\dAUC$ against
the same fibrosis-heterogeneity baseline, tested with the paired DeLong
statistic. Correcting the UW substrate while holding the cohort at
its original $144$ subjects leaves the estimate largely intact ($+0.093$,
$p=0.013$), so this was not principally a substrate artefact. Extending the
Roney arm from $62$ to the full $100$ released meshes is what collapses it, to
$\dAUC=+0.017$ ($p=0.452$). An effect that disappears when $38$ further real
subjects are added was a small-sample fluctuation, and it was one of
$\sim\!30$ DeLong comparisons besides. On the full cohort no comparison,
primary or secondary, shows SFI adding information beyond either baseline.

The discarded estimate is worth one more sentence, because its failure mode is
instructive. Under the sealed substrate the UW arm alone returned
$\dAUC=+0.340$ ($p=0.026$) on six positives, a value no one should have
believed and which we did not flag at the time; it is that arm which carried
the combined figure. A large point estimate resting on six events is a warning,
not a finding.

\paragraph{Naive (ungrouped) comparison.} The pre-registration asks for the naive
$\dAUC$ beside the grouped one so that leakage is visible, and we give it for
every real-cohort arm: Roney $+0.017$ (lr) and $-0.011$ (gbt), UW $+0.066$ and
$-0.160$, combined $-0.011$ and $+0.010$, against the grouped values of
Table~\ref{tab:null}. The gap has no consistent sign: the naive estimate runs
above the grouped one in two of the six arms, both logistic regression on a
single cohort, and below it in the other four including the pooled logistic
arm, so nothing here reproduces the optimism ungrouped cross-validation is
expected to show. On these cohorts each patient contributes
one row and is
its own group, so the grouping is close to a no-op.
The leakage effect is demonstrated instead on the replicated synthetic cohort,
where resampled networks share a parent shape family.

\paragraph{Power analysis.} In the synthetic cohort the subspace effect
($+0.0028$) needs $\sim\!4{,}150$ cases for $80\%$ power and the single-vector
effect ($+0.0007$) $\sim\!23{,}000$, clinically undetectable \emph{there}. We do
\emph{not} transport ``clinically undetectable'' to the real cohorts, which are a
separate regime. On the corrected substrate their estimates are $+0.009$ (Roney,
$n=100$) and $+0.012$ (combined, $n=182$), with intervals that contain zero and
exclude the $0.05$ gate. An earlier version read the then-current
$+0.036$--$0.051$ as compatible with a detectable effect we were underpowered to
confirm; those estimates are superseded and that reading no longer holds.

\subsection{Testing the redundancy explanation directly}
\label{sec:redundancy}
\noindent\emph{Source:}
\code{results/\allowbreak feature\allowbreak\_redundancy.json}.

The account above attributes the null to feature redundancy. That explanation was
inferred from the incremental AUC being zero, which does not establish it: a
feature that is pure noise also adds nothing, and so does one the classifier
cannot exploit. Those are three different diagnoses. We therefore measured
redundancy directly and without reference to the label, by regressing each SFI
column on the competitor block and by taking the leading canonical correlation
between the two blocks, with a permuted-SFI control to separate genuine
redundancy from a degenerate estimator.

The blocks are almost collinear in their dominant direction: the leading
canonical correlation between the nine SFI columns and the six competitors is
$0.996$, against $0.289$ for the permuted control. Taken alone that supports the
redundancy account. Per column it is less uniform, and the structure matters:

Four of the nine columns are reconstructible from the competitors at
$R^2\geq0.60$, and the two aggregate summaries almost perfectly so. The remaining
five keep most of their variance, at $R^2<0.25$, well above the permuted control
but far from redundant. The split does not fall along the aggregate versus
order-statistic line: what the competitors reconstruct is the two region-level
aggregates and the deeper region order statistics \code{sfi\_top2} and
\code{sfi\_top3}, and what resists reconstruction is the edge-level pair, the
region maximum and the region spread.

This qualifies rather than overturns the null, and it sharpens what the null
means. For \code{sfi\_total} and \code{sfi\_region\_mean} the competitors already
carry the information, and adding them cannot help. For \code{sfi\_region\_max},
\code{sfi\_top1} and \code{sfi\_region\_std} there is genuine unique variance in
the feature, and the finding is the stronger one: that variance exists and does
not predict the label. A biomarker can fail either by saying nothing new or by
saying something new that does not matter, and on this evidence SFI does both,
in different components. We had previously reported only the first.

\subsection{Region definition and the patient-level reduction}
\label{sec:regions}

The per-region SFI of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sfi} is stated over a region $R$, and until
now we had
left $R$ implicit. Since the partition and the reduction that follows it are
what actually produce the feature block whose incremental $\dAUC$ is the primary
endpoint, we give both explicitly. Neither was pre-registered --- the
pre-registration refers to the per-region SFI throughout without fixing a
partition or a patient-level reduction --- so both are a logged deviation, fixed
post hoc but without reference to the outcome, since SFI is closed-form given the
mesh and is never fitted to the label.

\paragraph{Partition.} Regions are a $3\times3$ grid over the universal atrial
coordinates. With $(\alpha,\beta)\in[0,1]^2$ per vertex, a vertex is assigned
$r=3\lfloor 3\beta\rfloor+\lfloor 3\alpha\rfloor \in\{0,\dots,8\}$, so every mesh
carries exactly nine regions regardless of its vertex count. An \emph{edge} is
assigned region $r$ only when both endpoints carry $r$; edges straddling two
regions go to a tenth bucket, $r=-1$, which is retained rather than discarded so
that the per-edge sensitivities sum to the global total. The rule is shared by
the closed-form and Monte-Carlo estimators so their region keys coincide.

This is where the two cohorts differ in kind and not only in degree. The Roney
meshes carry anatomically registered universal atrial coordinates, so their nine
regions are anatomical. The UW release ships none, so we substitute the top two
principal axes of the surface coordinates, min-max normalised. That surrogate is
a smooth two-dimensional chart adequate for binning, but it is not registered to
anatomy, and any region-level or localization claim on the UW cohort is
correspondingly weaker. The substitution is recorded in the mesh metadata rather
than left to the reader to infer.

\paragraph{Reduction to the patient.} The classification target is one binary
verdict per patient, so the nine per-region values must be reduced. Nine scalars
enter the design matrix, all in the \code{sfi\_} namespace: the global
\code{total} (the sum of expected per-edge $\lam_2$ drop over all edges);
\code{region\_max}, \code{region\_mean} and \code{region\_std} over the region
values; the three largest region values in descending order,
\code{top1}--\code{top3}; and \code{edge\_frag\_max} and \code{edge\_frag\_mean}
over the raw per-edge fragilities $(\phii_{2,i}-\phii_{2,j})^2$. The order
statistics are included because a fragility biomarker is a claim about the
\emph{worst} region rather than the average one, and reporting only a mean would
have made the hypothesis untestable in its own terms. Two of the nine names
denote one quantity: \code{region\_max} and \code{top1} are both the maximum over
the region values, and are identical on all $182$ subjects to zero difference, so
the block carries eight distinct columns under nine names. That is why they share
a row of the table in Section~\ref{sec:redundancy}.

Two properties of this construction matter for reading the null. First, the
competitor features are patient-level scalars already, so no analogous reduction
is applied to them; the comparison is between an add-on of eight distinct columns
and a six-column baseline, which if anything favours the add-on. Second, none of the
nine columns is fitted to the outcome. SFI is closed-form given the mesh and the
frozen uncoupling field, so it contributes no tuning degrees of freedom, and the
null cannot be attributed to a failure of feature selection.

\subsection{A provenance audit of the released substrates, and four controls}
\label{sec:substrate}
\noindent\emph{Sources}, all under \code{results/}: the cohort audit is
\code{cohort\allowbreak\_geometry\allowbreak\_audit.json}, and the four controls are
\code{uw\allowbreak\_substrate\allowbreak\_ablation.json},
\code{roney\allowbreak\_fibre\allowbreak\_control.json},
\code{roney\allowbreak\_fibrosis\allowbreak\_quantization.json} and
\code{fibrosis\allowbreak\_burden\allowbreak\_swap.json}. Every Roney inducibility
arm in this section runs on the $62$-mesh subset that predates the extension of
the predictive cohort to all $100$ released meshes, and was not re-run on the
full deposit; the predictive results of Section~\ref{sec:null} use all $100$.

The UW cohort forced this section. Under a protocol frozen before any label was
generated, and identical in every parameter, the Roney meshes returned $20/62$
subjects inducible ($32.3\%$) while the UW meshes returned $6/82$ ($7.3\%$) on
the substrate as released. Three UW rates recur below and are kept distinct
throughout: $6/82$ as released, $8/82$ after the cap repair --- the substrate
every predictive result uses --- and $14/82$ after both repairs. A
fourfold discrepancy on cohorts we had assumed carried comparable fibrosis
burden --- an assumption Control~4 below overturns --- is not a cohort property
one should accept; it is a hypothesis about the pipeline. We had
previously recorded the UW arm as uninformative and moved on, which was the
error. What follows is what a proper audit found, then three controls that do not
explain the anomaly, and a fourth that does.

\paragraph{The audit.} Two defects sit in the released files. The first is
ours. The only per-element scalar the UW release carries is \code{elemTag}, the
Dryad README does not define its values, and we had been mapping tag $164$ to
half-fibrotic tissue. Four measurements say it is not tissue at all. It resolves
into four to six large connected components in every one of the $82$ meshes,
five in $75$ of them, four in five and six in two, which is the ordinary
variation of pulmonary-vein anatomy: a left common trunk gives four, a right
middle vein gives six. Each large component is topologically a disc, $11$--$42$
mm across, sitting at $0.47$--$0.82$ of the atrial radius from the centroid. It
borders fibrotic elements on about $0.08\%$ of its incident edges against a
chance rate of $14$--$20\%$, a depletion beyond a hundredfold, which is
incompatible with reading it as a fibrosis border zone. And it is
ablation-invariant, holding at $20.0\%$ of elements before the procedure and
$19.9\%$ after, while every other class moves. Tag $164$ is the set of caps
sealing the pulmonary veins and the mitral valve. Removing it opens the surface:
the Euler characteristic falls by a median of $7$ (range $2$--$12$). We report
the \emph{change} rather than an opening count, because $\chi=2-k$ presumes a
clean closed surface and two of these meshes, ID040 and ID049, arrive at
$\chi=-16$ and $-18$ before anything is removed.

Treating those caps as half-conducting tissue does more than inflate fibrosis.
It seals the atrium's orifices, so simulated activation crosses the mitral valve
and the vein ostia instead of circling them, and reentry anchored on those
orifices, a principal mechanism of atrial fibrillation, cannot form.

The second defect is upstream. A \code{VECTORS fiber} array is present in all
$164$ released meshes, but it equals the constant $(1,0,0)$: the measured mean
directional spread is exactly $0.000$. The Roney cohort, by contrast, gives
$0.945$--$0.979$, with roughly one distinct direction per vertex. Conduction
anisotropy is applied relative to the \emph{local} fibre direction, so a single
global direction reduces it to a fixed coordinate bias carrying no anatomy. The
same cardinality argument applies to fibrosis: three distinct levels in the UW
release, effectively two once the caps are dropped, against $276$--$1071$ in
Roney.

\paragraph{Control 1: repairing the substrate.} Opening the orifices and
supplying a spatially varying fibre field, in a $2\times2$ design over all $82$
subjects (Fig.~\ref{fig:substrate}a), raises inducibility from $6/82$ to
$14/82$. Either repair alone converts two subjects ($6\to8$ in both single-factor arms)
and together they convert eight, so on these four cells the averaged main effect
of a repair and the interaction contrast are both $+4$ subjects and cannot be
told apart; we do not claim the two act super-additively. The mechanistic reading
an interaction would invite --- that reentry needs both an anatomical obstacle
and anisotropic heterogeneity --- is in any case undercut by Control~2 below,
which shows the fibre factor does not move the rate at all on a cohort that has a
real fibre field. We do not present this as a fix. The
strongest contrast does not reach paired significance (McNemar exact
$p=0.077$), it closes only $39\%$ of the distance to Roney, and only two of the
original six positives survive the repair. The varying fibre field is moreover a
probe, not a correction: it is the global $+z$ direction projected onto each
vertex tangent plane, and it is not anatomy.

\paragraph{Control 2: destroying the fibres on a cohort that has them.} The
cleaner experiment replaces the Roney fibre field with the same constant
$(1,0,0)$, holding anatomy, fibrosis and every solver parameter fixed
(Fig.~\ref{fig:substrate}b). The rate does not move at all: $20/62$ with real
fibres and $20/62$ without, McNemar $b=c=5$, $p=1.0$. Fibre degeneracy does not
drive the inducibility rate, and our first attribution of the UW anomaly to it
was wrong. The perturbation is real at the level of the dynamics --- sustained
reentry duration changes on $50/62$ subjects, by up to $661$ ms, and $10/62$
verdicts flip --- but those effects cancel in aggregate.

\paragraph{Control 3: destroying the fibrosis gradation.} The remaining
structural difference was that UW fibrosis is binary where Roney's is graded, and
graded border zones are where slow conduction and unidirectional block are
supposed to arise. Binarising the Roney fibrosis field should therefore have
suppressed reentry. It does the opposite (Fig.~\ref{fig:substrate}c). Thresholding
at $f>0.5$ raises inducibility from $20/62$ to $31/62$; thresholding at the
per-subject quantile that holds mean fibrosis burden fixed at $0.333$, so that
only the gradation is destroyed, raises it to $40/62$. Coarsening the fibrosis
field roughly doubles inducibility. In hindsight the mechanism is
unmysterious --- a sharp binary boundary is a stronger source--sink
discontinuity than a smooth ramp --- but the direction is the opposite of our
prediction, and it disqualifies coarse fibrosis as an explanation for a cohort
that is inducible \emph{too rarely}.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_substrate_audit.png}

Destroying the fibre field on a cohort that has one leaves the rate exactly

Taken together the three substrate controls leave the anomaly unexplained, and
they tighten rather than loosen it: of the two defects we can manipulate, one
does not move the rate at all and the other, imposed deliberately, moves it
upward. None of the three establishes an effect, which is weaker than
excluding one, and none of them points at a substrate \emph{defect} as the
cause. That is what redirected the search: from what might be broken in these
meshes to what they contain.

\paragraph{Control 4: the cohorts do not carry comparable fibrosis.} Having
exhausted the defects, we measured what the solver is actually given. Coarsened
to the same $2000$ nodes, mean fibrosis is $0.349$ on Roney and $0.244$ on UW,
and the fraction of tissue above half-fibrotic is $0.310$ against $0.239$. The
UW cohort carries roughly $30\%$ less fibrosis. The frozen protocol's own
calibration reports a rank correlation of $0.75$ between fibrosis and sustained
reentry, so a cohort with less of it should be less inducible. That is not an
anomaly; it is the labeller behaving as specified.

Swapping the burden between cohorts confirms it in both directions
(Fig.~\ref{fig:substrate}d). Rescaling Roney's fibrosis multiplicatively to UW's
level, which preserves the spatial pattern and the gradation and moves only the
level, collapses inducibility from $20/62$ to $1/62$ (Fisher exact
$p=4.0\times10^{-6}$). Rescaling UW's upward toward Roney's raises it from
$8/82$ to $21/82$ ($p=0.013$). Both control arms reproduce their native rates
exactly. The effect is large, symmetric, and an order of magnitude better
supported than any of the three substrate hypotheses.

One detail is worth stating because it bears on the original question. At
matched burden the point estimates invert: Roney rescaled to $0.241$ gives
$1/62$ while UW at $0.248$ gives $8/82$. On one and eight events that contrast
does not establish a reversal and we do not claim one; what it does show is that
the UW cohort is not intrinsically resistant to reentry. Most of the apparent
anomaly is accounted for by a difference in how much fibrosis the two released
datasets contain, and the two released defects, real as they are, were never
the explanation; what the burden account does not cover is openCARP's UW rate,
which the same rescaling leaves at $0/30$ (Section~\ref{sec:opencarp}). We had
spent three controls looking for something
broken before checking whether the two cohorts were comparable in the first
place, which is the more general lesson: an out-of-range aggregate is a
hypothesis about the pipeline, but it is a hypothesis about the inputs first.

\subsection{An independent solver as a robustness check}
\label{sec:opencarp}
\noindent\emph{Sources}, all under \code{results/}:
\code{opencarp\allowbreak\_full\allowbreak\_cohort.json}, with the
per-cohort and chance-corrected concordance in
\code{opencarp\allowbreak\_concordance\allowbreak\_detail.json};
all three calibration attempts, including the two rejected, are in
\code{opencarp\allowbreak\_calibration\allowbreak\_history.json},
with the discretisation and propagation checks in
\code{opencarp\allowbreak\_bin\allowbreak\_sensitivity.json} and
\code{opencarp\allowbreak\_uw\allowbreak\_propagation.json}.

Every in-silico result above rests on one phenomenological labeller, a monodomain
Mitchell--Schaeffer solver substituted for the pre-registered openCARP ground
truth when openCARP could not be installed. That substitution is now testable:
openCARP runs, and rather than replace the labeller with it --- which would
invalidate every number at once --- we run both and report their agreement.

\paragraph{Calibration.} openCARP ships the same Mitchell--Schaeffer membrane
model, so the comparison is between solvers rather than between physics, but its
defaults are not ours. Two quantities were matched to the frozen configuration,
each measured independently of the endpoint. Action-potential duration: openCARP's
default \code{tau\_close} $150$ gives APD90 $=277$~ms, against the frozen
$218$~ms healthy and $121$~ms fibrotic; measured on this build, \code{tau\_close}
$110\to209$~ms and $55\to113$~ms, so $115\times(1-0.5f)$ reproduces both within
about $2\%$. Conduction velocity: the shipped conductivity gives $0.344$~m/s
against the frozen band of $0.4$--$1.2$~m/s, which is a wavelength of roughly
$75$~mm in an atrium of $\sim\!110$~mm characteristic length and makes reentry far
too easy. Raising the intracellular conductivity alone saturates, because the
monodomain conductivity is the harmonic mean of the intra- and extracellular
values; scaling both gives $0.852$~m/s at $g_{il}=1.00$, and we adopt $1.05$.

Two configurations were rejected before this one, at $90\%$ and $76.7\%$
inducible with \emph{negative} fibrosis--verdict correlations, the signature of
rate-dependent block rather than reentry. We record that because it is the
protocol working: no endpoint parameter was adjusted at any stage, so the
calibration gate remained able to fail, and it did.

\paragraph{Result.} On the calibrated configuration the pre-registered gate is met
on Roney: $22/100$ inducible ($22.0\%$, inside the $10$--$40\%$ band) with a
fibrosis--verdict rank correlation of $+0.404$, against the frozen solver's $0.75$.
It is not met on UW, where openCARP returns $4/82$ inducible ($4.9\%$, below the
band) with a rank correlation of only $+0.079$.
Across all $182$ subjects the two solvers agree on $156$, or $85.7\%$. That figure
is raw rather than chance-corrected, and on an outcome this imbalanced the
correction matters: Cohen's $\kappa$ is $0.536$, and the two solvers agree on
only $21$ positives. Splitting it by cohort shows why the pooled number should
not be read as a tissue-level result. On Roney, where the gate is met, raw
agreement is $82/100$ ($82.0\%$) at $\kappa=0.561$; on UW, where it is not, raw
agreement is \emph{higher} at $74/82$ ($90.2\%$) but $\kappa$ falls to $0.287$,
because at a $4.9\%$ and $9.8\%$ event rate almost all of that agreement is
shared negatives. The pooled figure is therefore a statement about the protocol
across both cohorts rather than about the tissue, and the arm that looks most
concordant is the one carrying the least information. The
asymmetry is the substantive part. Of the $26$ subjects the two solvers disagree
on, openCARP calls only $5$ inducible where the monodomain solver does not, while
the reverse happens $21$ times; over the whole cohort openCARP labels $26/182$
against monodomain's $42/182$. That imbalance is systematic rather than sampling
noise (McNemar exact $p=0.0025$), so the two solvers differ in level and not only
in which subjects they pick. The stand-in is therefore not under-calling
relative to an independent reaction--diffusion solver --- it is the more liberal
of the two, which makes the incremental comparisons reported above conservative
rather than inflated. (On the $60$-subject calibration subset the discordance was
entirely one-way, $0$ against $5$; that does not hold on the full cohort and the
figure quoted here is the full-cohort one.)

\paragraph{What this check does not establish.} Two limits are worth stating. The
fibrosis field must be discretised into conductivity classes for openCARP, and the
verdict is unstable at five classes ($1/20$) though identical at ten, twenty and
forty ($7/20$, with complete per-subject agreement), so the operating choice of
twenty sits inside the converged regime but the coarse limit is not benign. More
importantly, openCARP returns $4/82$ on the UW cohort, and unlike the monodomain
solver this is \emph{not} recovered by raising that cohort's fibrosis burden:
rescaling UW upward leaves the rate at $0/30$. Two mundane explanations are
excluded. It is not a propagation failure --- on UW meshes the wave reaches
$99.4\%$ of nodes and peak depolarisation averages $0.83$, so activation spreads
across the surface and then dies, rather than never leaving the stimulus site.
Nor is it the coarser discretisation: UW meshes carry a mean edge of $3.18$~mm
against Roney's $2.64$~mm at the same node budget, but the convergence study shows
coarser meshes \emph{over}-call ($58.3\%$ at $1500$ nodes), so that difference
predicts a higher UW rate, not a near-zero one.

What remains is a genuine substrate difference that the burden rescaling does not
capture, and we do not know what it is. The monodomain burden result
(Section~\ref{sec:substrate}) is established in both directions on that solver and
is unaffected by this; what is not established is that openCARP's near-zero UW rate
has the same cause. We report the discrepancy rather than resolve it, and restrict
the concordance claim to the cohort where both solvers are calibrated. It is worth
noting which way this cuts: a robustness check that agreed everywhere would be less
informative than one that disagrees somewhere and says so.

\subsection{The labeller is reproducible in aggregate and unstable per subject}
\label{sec:labelnoise}

The controls expose something about the ground truth that matters more than the
substrate defects do. Destroying the Roney fibre field holds the aggregate rate
at exactly $20/62$ while flipping $10/62$ individual verdicts. Repairing the UW
substrate retains only two of its six original positives. The six-tier
convergence study reported below finds only $7/24$ subjects consistent across
resolutions. The aggregate inducibility rate is therefore a stable quantity, and
the per-subject verdict is not.

This is a statement about the endpoint, not an aside. The pre-registered
comparison is scored on per-subject labels, and non-differential noise in a
binary outcome attenuates any incremental AUC toward zero. Part of the null we
report is thus a property of the labeller rather than of the biomarker, and we
cannot cleanly separate the two with the present ground truth. It also sets a
floor on what any feature could achieve here, which is a limit on the whole
class of studies that score spectral or structural features against a
phenomenological inducibility verdict, not on SFI alone. The pre-registered
openCARP ground truth, substituted here for a monodomain Mitchell--Schaeffer
solver, would need to be run before the residual is attributable.

\subsection{The validity radius: why the single-vector estimator fails}
The relative error of the first-order SFI scales as $\Theta(\rhoval)$ and crosses
$10\%$ at $\rhoval^\ast\approx3$; real atria sit at median $\rhoval\approx2422$.
This proves the single-vector estimator is numerically invalid on real tissue
and $\phii_2$ is ill-conditioned, but (as stated in Section~\ref{sec:validity})
it does not by itself prove non-predictiveness; the predictive null of
Section~\ref{sec:null} is a separate, label-dependent finding, and
Section~\ref{sec:redundancy} shows redundancy accounts for only part of it.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$\rhoval$}{rho} scaling law}
\label{sec:scaling}
The gap collapses with system size by Weyl's law~\cite{weyl1912}: for a
$d$-dimensional domain the low Laplacian eigenvalues scale as $\lam_k\sim(k/N)^{2/d}$,
so $\rhoval$ grows lawfully with resolution.

also not resolved. What survives is
weaker: $\lvert\nabla\phii_2\rvert$ is a \emph{rank} localizer (the true origin

\subsection{Uncertainty quantification}
Morris elementary-effects screening (\code{results/e6\_metrics.json}) finds the
label most sensitive to diffusion $d_0$ ($\mu^\ast=500.7$), then fibrosis density
($147.8$) and transverse conductivity ($146.3$). A Gaussian-process surrogate has
leave-one-out $R^2=0.35$ (large residual anatomical/label variance beyond the
screened knobs, a label-noise caveat, not a positive). Across uncoupling
strengths $\Delta w\in\{0.18,0.36,0.54\}$ the grouped GBT
increment is $\dAUC=0.033$--$0.036$ with all $p\geq0.26$: below the
pre-registered $0.05$ gate, but non-significance on its own does not make these
arms null, and we report no interval for them, so they are indeterminate rather
than a demonstration that the synthetic null is robust to $\Delta w$.

\subsection{External inducibility-rate anchor (plausibility, not validation)}
Clinically observed AF inducibility spans $\sim\!5\%$ to $\sim\!85\%$ depending on
population, protocol aggressiveness, and the sustained-AF definition (Kumar et
al.\ 2012~\cite{kumar2012} report burst-sustained $14.8\%$ vs.\
decremental-sustained $41.2\%$ \emph{in the same patients}). Our labeller's Roney
$34.0\%$ ($34/100$) sits in the moderate-burst band (Marquardt et al.\
2018~\cite{marquardt2018} report $30.6\%$ in patients without prior AF), between
the no-AF-control floor ($5\%$, Oral et al.\ 2008~\cite{oral2008}) and the
AF/aggressive ceiling ($51\%$, Kawai et al.\ 2019~\cite{kawai2019}), consistent
with $\sim\!42\%$ post-ablation (Liu et al.\ 2020~\cite{liu2020}). The UW
$9.8\%$ ($8/82$ on the cap-corrected substrate that every predictive result
uses) sits just above the no-AF-control floor and far below the moderate-burst
band, for a cohort that \emph{is} AF patients.
We flag it as anomalous. Section~\ref{sec:substrate} traces it to a difference
in fibrosis burden between the two releases rather than to biology or to the
defects in the released UW substrate.
Two substrate defects are documented in the Limitations, and repairing both
moves the rate from $6/82$ to $14/82$, a difference that does not reach paired
significance (McNemar exact $p=0.077$, $n=82$) and that retains only two of the
six original positives; a direct control shows the fibre defect is not
responsible for any of it. This is a base-rate
\emph{plausibility check, not clinical validation}.

\subsection{Resolution convergence: the labeller does not converge in the tested window}
\label{sec:convergence}
Our own convergence study is the project's single weakest point, and we state it
before a reader finds it. On a completed six-tier study (Roney, $n=24$ at each of
1500/\allowbreak 3000/\allowbreak 6000/\allowbreak 12000/\allowbreak
24000/\allowbreak 48000 nodes; $144$ simulations,
\code{results/\allowbreak convergence\_summary\_roney.json}), the inducibility rate crashes
from the coarse mesh ($58.3\%$ at $1500$) to $16.7\%$ at $3000$, then appears
non-monotone across the fine tiers ($25.0\%$, $20.8\%$, $25.0\%$) and rises again
to $37.5\%$ at the finest affordable tier ($\sim\!50$k nodes), a
$17$-percentage-point spread over the last three tiers, so it has \emph{not}
settled.

How much of that pattern is real deserves stating, because $n=24$ per tier buys
little precision. Exact binomial intervals are
$[36.6,77.9]$, $[4.7,37.4]$, $[9.8,46.7]$, $[7.1,42.2]$, $[9.8,46.7]$ and
$[18.8,59.4]$ percent across the six tiers. These are marginal intervals: the
same $24$ subjects are simulated at every tier, so each describes one tier's own
precision and discards the pairing, and overlap between two of them is not a
test of their difference. We use them only for the negative claim. Every tier
from $3000$ nodes upward overlaps every other, so the apparent wiggle among the
fine tiers is \emph{not} distinguishable from sampling noise and we do not claim
it is a real non-monotonicity.

The paired comparison the intervals cannot make is available, because the same
$24$ subjects run at every tier: for each adjacent pair the $2\times2$ table is
fixed by the two marginals and the agreement count, so an exact McNemar test
follows without any further simulation
(\code{results/\allowbreak convergence\allowbreak\_paired\allowbreak\_tests.json}).
It separates the two halves of the pattern cleanly. The coarse-mesh drop is real:
$11$ subjects lose their verdict between $1500$ and $3000$ nodes against $1$ that
gains one, $p=0.0063$, and it survives Holm correction across the five adjacent
comparisons ($p=0.032$). None of the four fine-tier transitions is resolvable
($p=0.375$ to $1.000$ unadjusted, all $1.000$ after correction). What the study
establishes is therefore sharper than the intervals alone allow, and still
sufficient for our purpose: the coarsest tier demonstrably over-calls, while
across the fine tiers the rate cannot be shown to have converged and no tier can
be declared the settled value. A study that could distinguish these tiers
would need several hundred subjects per tier, which is roughly two orders of
magnitude more simulation than the $144$ runs here.

The convergence finding limits the absolute numbers but not the comparative
claims. The rate does \emph{not}
collapse to zero; the phenomenon is real; it is the \emph{magnitude} that is
resolution-sensitive up to $\sim\!50$k nodes. Because the main results ran at
$2{,}000$ nodes, every \emph{absolute} number (inducibility rate, competitor AUC,
localizer origins) is coarse-mesh-provisional pending the deferred openCARP
validation. What this does \emph{not} touch is what we lead with: (a)~the
\emph{relative} SFI-vs-competitor comparison, since both arms score the
\emph{same} labels, so the shift in \emph{absolute} AUC is common to them; and
(b)~the label-free estimator-validity argument ($\rhoval$ is computed from the
graph Laplacian, no labels at all). Sharing labels does not make the relative
comparison unbiased: per-subject label noise attenuates the incremental $\dAUC$
toward zero (Section~\ref{sec:labelnoise}), so that comparison is conservative.

%======================================================================
\section{Discussion}
%======================================================================
This work makes four contributions.
(1)~A pre-registered, leakage-controlled, effect-size-gated evaluation of a
plausible spectral biomarker: a clean null at synthetic scale ($10^5$ networks),
where the mechanism remains inferred, and a measured null on $182$ patients of
real anatomy, where it is measured rather than inferred
(Section~\ref{sec:redundancy}, on those same $182$ subjects: the aggregate SFI
summaries are reconstructible from the competitors at $R^2=0.970$, the extreme and
edge-level columns are not, and their unique variance is unpredictive).
(2)~A label-free validity criterion $\rhoval$ for the single-vector estimator:
it proves $\phii_2$ is ill-conditioned on real tissue ($\rhoval\approx2422\gg
\rhoval^\ast\approx3$), a portable a priori check for any Fiedler-based marker,
with the caveat that $\rhoval$ bounds estimator accuracy, not predictive
content. (3)~A weak-but-real, purely connectivity-derived rank localizer that
beats centrality baselines across three media but does not beat substrate
localization, which is the better localizer in the FHN arm
(Table~\ref{tab:localize}); the strict pre-registered localization endpoint is
null.
(4)~A demonstration that standard methodology manufactures the exact false
positives this protocol catches (leakage $+0.117$ AUC; the $p$-value trap).

\subsection{Four corrections, and what caught each}
\label{sec:corrections}

Four claims in earlier versions of this work did not survive, and because they
are referred to individually where they arise, we set them out together here.

The first was a positive result. A pre-specified additional feature-set
comparison reported that SFI beat a fibrosis-heterogeneity baseline on the
combined real cohort
($\dAUC=+0.103$, $p=0.009$), which we read as showing SFI is not merely
re-encoded fibrosis. Extending the Roney arm from $62$ to all $100$ released
meshes reduced it to $+0.017$ ($p=0.452$). What caught it was more data, and what
made the original vulnerable was that it rested largely on one cohort's
$+0.340$ over a near-chance baseline on six events.

The second was a causal attribution. We reported that the degenerate fibre field
in the UW release was the likely reason that cohort is so rarely inducible. What
caught it was a direct control: destroying the fibre field on a cohort that has an
intact one left the inducibility rate at exactly $20/62$, $p=1.0$. The
explanation was plausible, mechanistically motivated, and wrong, and no amount of
further argument would have revealed that.

The third was a set of geometric figures. We stated that element tag $164$ formed
exactly five components, had exactly zero adjacency to fibrosis, and left Euler
characteristic $-3$ on removal. All three were generalised from a single mesh or
from a rounded mean. What caught them was writing regression tests over the whole
cohort, two of which failed on first run. The identification of tag $164$ itself
survived and is better supported by the census than by the original figures.

The fourth was the primary endpoint itself. An earlier version reported a
combined real-cohort $\dAUC$ of $+0.051$ and read the endpoint as missed only on
significance. Correcting the UW substrate at the original $144$ subjects brought
that to $+0.036$; extending the Roney arm from $62$ to all $100$ released meshes
brought it to $+0.012$, so the larger share of the drop was sample size rather
than substrate. What caught it was the substrate audit followed by the cohort
extension (Section~\ref{sec:null}).

We note the pattern rather than each instance. Every one was caught by a control,
a larger sample, or a test that this project designed and ran against its own
results before submission; none was found by a reader. The first correction is
also a worked example of what the paper argues elsewhere. Naive methodology --- a
$p<0.05$ threshold with no effect-size gate, no multiplicity accounting and no
replication on more data --- would have reported $\dAUC=+0.103$ at $p=0.009$ as a
finding. It was produced by our own pipeline and it was false, and the protocol
that rejected it is the same one applied to the primary endpoint throughout.

The validity screen is
domain-general. Spectral-graph fragility markers are used for seizure-focus
localization, brain connectomics, and power-grid vulnerability. The same
precondition applies: compute $\rhoval=\lVert\Delta L\rVert/(\lam_3-\lam_2)$; if
$\rhoval\gg1$, the first-order spectral marker is outside its validity radius, so
its \emph{numerical} $\Delta\lam_2$ estimates and the stability of $\phii_2$
cannot be trusted. That is a statement about the estimator, not about whether the
feature ranks cases usefully, which must still be tested against competitors.

We are equally explicit about what we do not claim. The mathematics is
classical; no new theorem.
$\rhoval$ does \emph{not} prove SFI is non-predictive, only that the
single-vector estimator is ill-conditioned; the predictive null is separate from
it, and is only partly redundancy (Section~\ref{sec:redundancy}). The real-cohort
result \emph{is} a measured null in the sense that the interval excludes the
pre-registered gate, but it does \emph{not} exclude a small effect: the lower
bound is $-0.017$, so an increment of a few thousandths remains compatible with
the data and would need a far larger cohort to resolve. The localizer is
\emph{not} precise (strict endpoint null) and does
\emph{not} beat substrate imaging. The $\rhoval$ scaling ``law'' is a clean match
to Weyl only on the real atrial 2-manifold. Labels are a monodomain simulator,
not clinical outcomes and not openCARP.

%======================================================================
\section{Limitations}
%======================================================================
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item The labeller is strongly resolution-sensitive and does not converge in the
tested window (Section~\ref{sec:convergence}):
the rate drops from $58\%$ at $1500$ nodes to $17\%$ at $3000$ and rises to
$37.5\%$ at $\sim\!50$k nodes, above the
$34.0\%$ rate at the $2{,}000$-node operating resolution, so the direction of the
residual bias there is not established. At $n=24$ per tier the intervals overlap
for every tier from $3000$ nodes upward, so no ordering among the fine tiers is
resolvable and we do not claim the wiggle there is real; only $7/24$
subjects are fully consistent across all six tiers. All \emph{absolute} rates and
AUCs are coarse-mesh-provisional; the \emph{relative} comparison scores the same
labels in both arms, so the shift is common to them, though per-subject label
noise still attenuates the incremental $\dAUC$ toward zero.
\item Simulator, not clinical, labels: the pre-registered openCARP ground truth
was substituted with monodomain Mitchell--Schaeffer, a logged deviation.
\item A clinical endpoint family \emph{is} pre-registered and is deliberately not
reported here. Per-patient two-year post-ablation recurrence outcomes for the $82$
UW patients were shared with us after the simulator analysis was complete, and
\S8 of the pre-registration was frozen and tagged before any feature was placed
alongside the outcome column; at the time of writing no such association has been
computed. Two things follow that we state now rather than after seeing an answer.
The outcome column is restricted-use human-subject data, not covered by the
CC0 that applies to the meshes, so it is not redistributed with this work and
only aggregate statistics derived from it may be published. And the study is
underpowered for that endpoint by construction: on the observed $48/82$ event
rate, power at the $\dAUC\geq0.05$ gate used for the simulator endpoint is
$0.24$ at best and $0.13$ under a weaker shared-noise assumption, so that gate
is \emph{not} reused there. The minimum detectable effect is $\dAUC\approx0.10$
to $0.15$, and $80\%$ power at $0.05$ would need $371$ to $859$ patients
(\code{results/\allowbreak power\allowbreak\_real\allowbreak\_outcomes.json}).
A null below that band will be reported as inconclusive, never as evidence that
SFI adds nothing clinically.
\item The UW cohort's released substrate carries two defects, detailed with their
controls in Section~\ref{sec:substrate}: a fibre field that is a constant vector
in all $164$ meshes, and element tag $164$, which is the caps over the atrial
orifices and which we had been treating as half-fibrotic tissue. Repairing both
raises inducibility only from $6/82$ to $14/82$, and none of the three
substrate explanations for the cohort's low rate is supported by direct
control; Control~4 attributes the low rate instead to a fibrosis burden
roughly $30\%$ lower
($0.244$ against Roney's $0.349$), reproduced by swapping the burden in either
direction. Its regions additionally rest on a PCA surrogate rather than
registered atrial coordinates. The cohort's predictive test is uninformative
about SFI, and what remains open is narrower: openCARP returns $4/82$ here and
the same rescaling does not recover it ($0/30$, Section~\ref{sec:opencarp}).
\item The inducibility label is stable in aggregate but noisy per subject, which
attenuates every $\dAUC$ we report. Destroying the Roney fibre field holds the rate
at $20/62$ yet flips $10/62$ individual verdicts; repairing the UW substrate retains
only $2$ of its $6$ original positives. That compounds the mesh-convergence
instability above, where just $7/24$ subjects were consistent across six
resolutions. Non-differential label noise biases incremental AUC toward zero, so the
null we report is partly a statement about the labeller and not only about SFI.
\item $\sim\!30$ DeLong comparisons were run on the real cohorts. The
pre-registration provides no multiplicity plan for that family, so every
$p$-value we report is unadjusted; on the full $182$-subject corrected analysis
none reaches the pre-registered $\alpha=0.05$. The smallest is $p=0.121$
(\code{fibrosis\_vs\_+SFI}, gradient boosting, UW), and that one sits on a base AUC
of $0.562$ with eight positives, so it is uninformative rather than borderline. An
earlier version reported this family's combined estimate as the study's one
positive at $p=0.009$; it is withdrawn (Section~\ref{sec:corrections}).
\item $\rhoval^\ast$ and the random-geometric gap exponents are empirical; only
the real atrial 2-manifold cleanly matches the Weyl exponent.
\end{enumerate}

%======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%======================================================================
A closed-form spectral fragility index does not add practically meaningful
reentry-inducibility prediction beyond existing features, either at synthetic
scale or on $182$ patients of real anatomy, where the combined estimate is
$+0.012$ with an interval that excludes the pre-registered gate. The mechanism is
decomposable and only half of it is redundancy: the aggregate SFI summaries are
reconstructible from the competitor set at $R^2=0.970$, while the extreme and
edge-level columns carry variance of their own that simply does not predict
the label. Independently
of the predictive question, the single-vector estimator is ill-conditioned on real
tissue ($\rhoval\gg1$), and lawfully more so as meshes are refined.

The core result is not a discovery but a carefully-scoped boundary: where a
Fiedler-based estimator is numerically valid, why real tissue lies far outside it,
and how readily standard methodology would have reported a working biomarker --- a
false positive this project produced from its own pipeline, at $\dAUC=+0.103$ and
$p=0.009$, and then withdrew. What the study cannot settle is the absolute
inducibility rate, which does not converge within the mesh resolutions we could
afford; the comparative claims, which score identical labels in both arms, do not
depend on it. An independent solver run alongside the labeller agrees with it on
$85.7\%$ of subjects and is the more conservative of the two, which bounds how
much of the null could be an artefact of the stand-in.

\section{Formal (proof-assistant) verification of the SFI algebraic core}
\label{app:formal}
%======================================================================
The algebraic core of the SFI edge-sensitivity identity is re-proved inside the
Coq 8.18 proof assistant and checked by its small trusted kernel, an assurance
level above a computer-algebra system. The primary file
(\code{formal/sfi\_edge\_identity\_Q.v}) works over the exact rationals
$\mathbb{Q}$, so \code{Print Assumptions} reports \emph{``Closed under the global
context''} for every theorem: zero axioms. A mathematically identical
companion over the reals $\mathbb{R}$ (\code{formal/sfi\_edge\_identity.v})
inherits Coq's two standard real-number-library axioms and is kept only to show
the statements hold over $\mathbb{R}$.
