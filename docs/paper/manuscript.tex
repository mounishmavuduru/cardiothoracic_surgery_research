\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We ask whether a closed-form spectral graph biomarker predicts reentry
inducibility in atrial tissue beyond established fibrosis and connectivity
features. The biomarker is the \textbf{Spectral Fragility Index (SFI)}, the
Fiedler-value sensitivity $\partial\lam_2/\partial w_{ij}=(\phii_i-\phii_j)^2$
aggregated under a fibrosis-weighted conduction-uncoupling field. Pre-registered
before any labels were seen, the primary endpoint was $\dAUC\geq 0.05$ with a
DeLong paired $p<0.05$ on shape-family-held-out folds. The endpoint is not met,
but the strength of the negative differs by regime, and we report the two
regimes separately. On up to $100{,}000$ synthetic excitable networks SFI adds
no practically meaningful predictive value (subspace $\dAUC\approx+0.003$;
$\sim\!4{,}150$ cases needed at $80\%$ power, resolvable only because $N$ is
huge, and so clinically undetectable there). On two real cohorts ($182$
patients: Roney $n=100$, UW/Boyle $n=82$) the combined gradient-boosting
estimate is $+0.012$ with a bootstrap interval $[-0.017,+0.044]$ that excludes
the threshold, so the real-anatomy result is a measured null and not a
shortfall of power. An earlier version of this work reported $+0.051$ here and
called the real-cohort evidence inconclusive. Correcting how we had read one
cohort's released substrate, which we found only afterwards, moved that figure
to $+0.036$ at the original cohort size, and extending the Roney arm from $62$
to all $100$ released meshes moved it to $+0.012$, so most of the drop was
sample size, not substrate. The audit that found the misreading, and the four
controls that followed, are reported in full; the cause of that cohort's
unusually low inducibility rate proved to be a $25\%$ difference in fibrosis
burden between the two released cohorts rather than any substrate defect, and
that difference accounts for the apparent anomaly and reproduces it in either
direction when the burden is swapped. Where the null is clean, only half the
mechanism is feature redundancy: the aggregate SFI summaries are reconstructible
from the competitor set at $R^2=0.970$, while the extreme and edge-level columns
retain variance of their own ($R^2<0.25$) and still do not predict the label.
The exact per-region $\Delta\lam_2$ SFI, which has no first-order approximation
error, also adds nothing over the competitor set. A separate, label-free result
explains why the \emph{single-vector} SFI is also ill-conditioned on real
tissue: it is valid only while
$\rhoval=\lVert\Delta L\rVert/(\lam_3-\lam_2)=O(1)$, and real atria have
median $\rhoval\approx 2422$, at least $272\times$ past the empirical
$\rhoval^\ast\approx 3$ on every mesh tested, so by Davis--Kahan the Fiedler
vector is unstable. We do \emph{not} claim $\rhoval$ proves non-predictiveness;
it bounds the accuracy of the $\Delta\lam_2$ \emph{magnitude}, not the
classification content of a ranking feature. The spectral gap of the weighted
conduction Laplacian collapses as $N^{-1.00}$ on the real atrial surface,
matching the 2-manifold Weyl exponent, so $\rhoval$ grows lawfully with
resolution. One component partially survives: the
Fiedler-gradient localizer $\lvert\nabla\phii_2\rvert$ is a weak but real rank
localizer (origin in the top $\sim\!18\%$ on synthetic networks, and weaker on
real anatomy, where only $\sim\!20$ origins are available) that beats
graph-centrality baselines and transfers across three dynamical media, though it
co-localizes with substrate imaging rather than beating it, and substrate
imaging is the better localizer in the FHN arm ($\sim\!0.10$ against $0.176$),
for a reason we make precise. Naive methodology (random CV, $p$-value
thresholds, no effect-size gate) would have reported SFI as working, whereas our
pre-registered protocol rejects it. Our own convergence study identifies a key
limitation: the inducibility labeller is strongly resolution-sensitive and, on a
completed six-tier study ($1500$--$48000$ nodes, $n=24$), does not converge
within the tested window; the direction of the residual bias at the
$2{,}000$-node operating resolution is not established, since the finest tier
($37.5\%$) sits above the operating-resolution rate ($34.0\%$). A pre-registered
clinical endpoint on two-year recurrence outcomes for the $82$ UW patients
supplies the study's sharpest illustration: its gate fired, at $\dAUC=+0.197$
with $p=0.0061$, and the analysis plan frozen before the outcomes were joined
identifies the result as an artefact of an anti-predictive baseline (AUC
$0.250$), which nine columns of random noise reproduce (empirical $p=0.333$);
the endpoint is not met. Our contribution is a carefully bounded negative
result: the mechanism behind it, a label-free criterion for the estimator's
validity, a characterization of how it scales, and a demonstration of the false
positives that standard methodology produces, including one this protocol caught
on real patients.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%======================================================================
Algebraic connectivity $\lam_2$ (Fiedler 1973~\cite{fiedler1973}) governs the
stability of coupled excitable and oscillator networks (the
master-stability-function result~\cite{pecora1998} and the synchronizability
eigenratio $\lam_N/\lam_2$~\cite{barahona2002}), and has been used as a
real-time seizure detector~\cite{bomela2020}. The per-edge sensitivity
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\lam_2}{\partial w_{ij}}=(\phii_{2,i}-\phii_{2,j})^2
\label{eq:edge}
\end{equation}
is a classical identity underlying Fiedler-vector edge-editing
heuristics~\cite{ghosh2006}. It is therefore natural to hypothesize that
aggregating this sensitivity over a fibrosis-weighted perioperative uncoupling
field yields a per-region biomarker of atrial-fibrillation (AF) reentry
fragility. We test this hypothesis, and we pre-register the null
(``SFI merely re-encodes fibrosis'') as an accepted, reportable outcome, so
that a negative result counts as a success of the protocol and not as a
file-drawer casualty.

The mathematics we use is classical, and we claim no new theorem. Our
contributions are
(i)~a leakage-controlled incremental test of SFI against real competitors and a
spatial null on real anatomy; (ii)~a label-free validity criterion and a
measured gap-scaling exponent that
matches the Weyl 2-manifold prediction on real atria, which together explain why
the \emph{single-vector estimator} is ill-conditioned on real tissue and why any
first-order Fiedler-based marker faces the same conditioning problem there, and
which do not explain the predictive null; (iii)~a cross-medium transfer analysis
isolating the one
component that generalizes; and (iv)~a falsification-protocol demonstration
showing that standard methodology would have manufactured exactly the false
positive this protocol catches.

In brief, a closed-form spectral
fragility index does \emph{not} add practically meaningful reentry-inducibility
prediction beyond existing features, either at synthetic scale or on $182$
patients of real anatomy. The mechanism is partly feature redundancy and partly a
feature that is genuinely new but unpredictive (Section~\ref{sec:redundancy}).
Independently of that null, the estimator's own conditioning fails there
($\rhoval\gg 1$). What we report is a carefully drawn boundary rather than a
discovery: where a Fiedler-based estimator is numerically valid, why real tissue
lies far outside it, and how readily naive methodology mistakes redundant,
inconclusive signal for a working biomarker.

\section{Theory: the Spectral Fragility Index}
\label{sec:theory}
%======================================================================
The core mathematics is classical numerical linear algebra, and the contribution
lies in its use: each result is stated together with its prior art
(Section~\ref{sec:credits}), and the validity boundary is made explicit
(Section~\ref{sec:validity}).

\subsection{The weighted conduction graph}
Nodes are mesh vertices and edges are triangle edges, with the edge weight
$w_{ij}>0$ a fibre-anisotropic conduction conductance attenuated by local
fibrosis. The combinatorial Laplacian is
\begin{equation}
L=D-W,\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}\Big(\textstyle\sum_j w_{ij}\Big).
\end{equation}
$L$ is symmetric positive-semidefinite with a single zero eigenvalue (the
constant vector) for a connected graph, with spectrum
$0=\lam_1<\lam_2\leq\lam_3\leq\cdots$ and eigenvectors $\phii_1,\phii_2,\dots$;
$\lam_2$ is the algebraic connectivity and $\phii_2$ the Fiedler vector.

\subsection{The closed-form fragility (first order)}
$L$ is a sum of rank-one edge terms, so
\begin{equation}
L=\sum_{(i,j)\in E} w_{ij}\,(e_i-e_j)(e_i-e_j)^{\!\top}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\frac{\partial L}{\partial w_{ij}}=(e_i-e_j)(e_i-e_j)^{\!\top}.
\label{eq:dL}
\end{equation}
For a \emph{simple} eigenvalue $\lam$ with unit eigenvector $\phii$,
first-order (Hellmann--Feynman) perturbation theory gives
$\partial\lam/\partial w_{ij}=\phii^{\!\top}(\partial L/\partial w_{ij})\phii
=(\phii_i-\phii_j)^2$, which for the Fiedler pair is Eq.~\eqref{eq:edge}: a
non-negative per-edge \emph{fragility}. Reducing a conductance $w_{ij}$ by a
small $\Delta w_{ij}\geq 0$ (uncoupling) lowers $\lam_2$ by
$\Delta w_{ij}(\phii_{2,i}-\phii_{2,j})^2$ to first order.

The identity is verified in six independent ways. In computer algebra,
(i)~sympy proves Eqs.~\eqref{eq:edge}--\eqref{eq:dL}
symbolically, and (ii)~on a concrete $5$-node graph the finite-difference
$\partial\lam_2/\partial w_{ij}$ matches $(\phii_{2,i}-\phii_{2,j})^2$ to
$\lvert\text{err}\rvert\approx 2\times10^{-17}$ (mpmath, $40$ digits).
Exactness and implementation independence are checked separately:
(iii)~the identity holds exactly with rational weights (zero floating
point); (iv)~an interval-arithmetic enclosure certifies the true $\lam_2$ to
within $\sim\!4\times10^{-40}$; and (v)~$\lam_2/\phii_2$ agree across scipy,
networkx, and a
hand-rolled inverse iteration (spread $2.5\times10^{-15}$). These three
tolerances are printed at run time by \code{scripts/verify\_sfi\_identity.py} and
\code{scripts/verify\_math\_rigor.py} rather than stored in \code{results/}, so
they are reproduced by rerunning those scripts. Finally, (vi)~the
algebraic core is kernel-checked in the Coq proof assistant over the
exact rationals, axiom-free (Appendix~\ref{app:formal}). The analytic
Hellmann--Feynman step (differentiability of a simple eigenvalue) is the cited
classical result~\cite{ghosh2006}, not re-proved here.

\subsection{Per-region aggregation: the SFI}
Model diffuse perioperative stress as a distribution of small, non-negative,
fibrosis-weighted fractional conductance reductions $\Delta w$ (mean
$\mathbb{E}[\Delta w_{ij}]=\mathrm{frac}_{ij}\,w_{ij}$, calibrated to literature
perioperative conduction-velocity slowing; $\Delta w\approx 0.36$, frozen in the
pre-registration). The per-region Spectral Fragility Index is the expected
$\lam_2$ drop contributed by region $R$:
\begin{equation}
\SFI(R)=\mathbb{E}[\Delta\lam_2\text{ from }R]
\approx\underbrace{\sum_{(i,j)\in R}\mathbb{E}[\Delta w_{ij}]\,(\phii_{2,i}-\phii_{2,j})^2}_{\text{first order}}
-\underbrace{\sum_{k\neq2}\frac{(\phii_k^{\!\top}\Delta
L\,\phii_2)^2}{\lam_2-\lam_k}}_{\text{2nd-order resolvent}}
+O(\lVert\Delta L\rVert^3).
\label{eq:sfi}
\end{equation}
The resolvent term is \emph{subtracted} because the left-hand side is a drop,
under $L'=L-\Delta L$; since every denominator $\lam_2-\lam_k$ with $k\geq3$ is
negative and the $k=1$ term vanishes ($\Delta L$ annihilates the constant vector
$\phii_1$), that subtracted sum is itself negative, so the true drop
\emph{exceeds} the first-order estimate --- which is what the validity sweep measures, with the
exact $\Delta\lam_2$ above the first-order prediction at every $\rhoval$.
The second-order term is the source of the validity radius developed below. Near
eigenvalue degeneracy ($\lam_2\approx\lam_3$) the single $\phii_2$ is an
arbitrary rotation in the low invariant subspace, so we use a basis-independent
\emph{subspace (projector) SFI}: with $P=\sum_c\phii_c\phii_c^{\!\top}$ over the
clustered subspace, $s_{ij}^{\!\top}Ps_{ij}=\sum_c(\phii_{c,i}-\phii_{c,j})^2$
($s_{ij}=e_i-e_j$) is well-defined. For discrete edits (Maze lesion,
atriotomy) the expansion is abandoned and $\lam_2$ is recomputed exactly
after the rank-one edit.

\subsection{The validity radius: the central caveat}
\label{sec:validity}
The first-order form is trustworthy only while the perturbation is small
relative to the spectral gap $\lam_3-\lam_2$. Weyl's inequality and the
Davis--Kahan theorem bound different quantities, and applying Weyl to both is a
common error:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item Weyl's inequality bounds the \emph{eigenvalue} move:
      $\lvert\Delta\lam_2\rvert\leq\lVert\Delta L\rVert$.
\item Davis--Kahan~\cite{daviskahan1970} bounds the
      \emph{eigenvector} move, which scales like
      $\lVert\Delta L\rVert/(\lam_3-\lam_2)$; this ratio, and not the Weyl
      bound, governs when $\phii_2$ (hence the single-vector SFI) degrades.
\end{itemize}
Define the \emph{validity radius}
\begin{equation}
\rhoval:=\frac{\lVert\Delta L\rVert}{\lam_3-\lam_2}.
\end{equation}
From second-order Rayleigh--Schr\"odinger theory the relative error of the
first-order $\Delta\lam_2$ prediction is $\Theta(\rhoval)$, and the Fiedler
vector rotates by $\sin\angle(\phii_2,\phii_2')\leq\sqrt{2}\,\rhoval$
(Davis--Kahan). Empirically the error is $1\%$ at $\rhoval=0.3$, crosses $10\%$
at $\rhoval^\ast\approx 3$, and reaches $92\%$ at $\rhoval=28$, with Weyl holding
throughout. On real excitable media the spectral gap is small and $\rhoval$ is
correspondingly large: over the $12$ scanned meshes the median is
$\rhoval\approx2422$ with an interquartile range of $[1082,\,7384]$ and a full
range of $[817,\,16554]$. The spread is wide because $\rhoval$ inherits the
near-degenerate gap in its denominator, but every mesh exceeds $\rhoval^\ast$ and
even the smallest does so by a factor of $272$, so the conclusion does not rest
on the median, which itself sits about $10^3\times$ past $\rhoval^\ast$.

The criterion $\rhoval$ proves that the
\emph{single-vector} first-order SFI is a numerically invalid estimator of
$\Delta\lam_2$ on real tissue, and that $\phii_2$ is ill-conditioned
(Davis--Kahan), a label-free statement about the estimator. It does
\emph{not} prove SFI is non-predictive: $\rhoval$ bounds the accuracy of the
$\Delta\lam_2$ magnitude, not the classification content of a monotone
ranking feature; indeed the exact $\Delta\lam_2$ SFI (no $\rhoval$
limitation) is also non-predictive (Section~\ref{sec:null}), so the
predictive null has a separate cause, which Section~\ref{sec:redundancy} measures
and finds to be only half redundancy. The label-free status of $\rhoval$ is only
partial, since its numerator carries the fibrosis-weighted $\Delta w$ field and
only its denominator $\lam_3-\lam_2$ is truly label-free; and because
$\rhoval^\ast\approx3$ is calibrated on a $16$-node path graph with a
problem-dependent constant, ``$10^3\times$ past $\rhoval^\ast$'' is
order-of-magnitude, not exact.

\subsection{Prior art: the mathematics is not new}
\label{sec:credits}
The identity~\eqref{eq:edge} is Ghosh \& Boyd (2006)~\cite{ghosh2006}; algebraic
connectivity is Fiedler (1973)~\cite{fiedler1973}; $\lam_2$ governing coupled
excitable/oscillator stability is Pecora \& Carroll
(1998)~\cite{pecora1998} with the eigenratio of Barahona \& Pecora
(2002)~\cite{barahona2002}; Weyl's law, Davis--Kahan, and second-order
perturbation theory are classical (1970s and earlier). No result in
Section~\ref{sec:theory} is a new theorem, and none is presented as one. The
contribution is recognizing that $\rhoval$ is the right \emph{a priori}
validity screen for a spectral fragility biomarker, measuring its violation on
real tissue, and using it to mechanistically explain a negative result.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
%======================================================================
Full detail is in the pre-registration (\code{docs/\allowbreak
PRE\_REGISTRATION.md}) and
theory note (\code{docs/\allowbreak SFI\_THEORY.md}); the endpoints,
cross-validation scheme,
baseline set, and accepted null were frozen before any labels were inspected.

\paragraph{Reporting guideline.} The pre-registered clinical analysis of
Section~\ref{sec:clinical} --- the prediction of two-year post-ablation
arrhythmia recurrence in the $82$ University of Washington patients, and the
only analysis in this work scored against a real patient outcome --- is
reported in accordance with the \textbf{TRIPOD+AI} statement (Transparent
Reporting of a multivariable prediction model for Individual Prognosis Or
Diagnosis, updated for prediction models developed with regression or machine
learning methods)~\cite{tripodai2024}. A completed TRIPOD+AI checklist
accompanies this manuscript as a supplement. TRIPOD+AI supersedes the original
TRIPOD statement, which its authors state should no longer be used, and it is
the applicable version here because the clinical analysis reports both a
penalised logistic regression and a gradient-boosted tree ensemble.

The scope of that adherence is stated deliberately rather than left to
inference. Every other endpoint in this paper is in silico: the outcome is an
electrophysiology simulator's reentry-inducibility verdict, not a patient
outcome, so those analyses fall outside TRIPOD+AI's remit and no claim of
adherence is made for them. They are nonetheless reported against the same
frozen protocol, and the checklist marks each item accordingly instead of
recording compliance the design cannot support. STARD was considered and judged
inapplicable: this work evaluates the incremental discrimination of a
multivariable prediction model, not the accuracy of an index test against a
reference standard, and reports no sensitivity, specificity, or predictive
values at a diagnostic threshold.

\paragraph{The edge weight.} Everything downstream ($\lam_2$, the Fiedler
vector, the spectral gap, $\rhoval$ and the SFI itself) is a function of the
conduction graph's edge weights, so we give their form explicitly. For an edge
$(i,j)$ let $\hat{e}_{ij}$ be the unit vector along it, $\bar{f}_{ij}$ the
normalised mean of the two endpoint fibre directions, and
$a_{ij}=\lvert\hat{e}_{ij}\cdot\bar{f}_{ij}\rvert$ their absolute alignment. With
$\phi_{ij}=\tfrac12(\phi_i+\phi_j)$ the mean endpoint fibrosis,
\begin{equation}
w_{ij}=\underbrace{\bigl[c_\perp+(c_\parallel-c_\perp)\,a_{ij}^{2}\bigr]}_{\text{anisotropy}}
\cdot\underbrace{\mathrm{clip}\bigl(1-\phi_{ij},\,\varepsilon,\,1\bigr)}_{\text{fibrosis}},
\label{eq:weight}
\end{equation}
with $c_\parallel=1.0$ along fibre, $c_\perp=0.3$ across, and a floor
$\varepsilon=0.05$ so dense scar retains a small residual conductance instead of
disconnecting the graph. The $a^{2}$ dependence makes the weight a function of
alignment and not of direction, so it is invariant to fibre sign, which is
the only property of $\bar{f}$ that is identifiable. Both factors are bounded, so
$w_{ij}\in[\varepsilon c_\perp,\,c_\parallel]$; no edge weight can vanish and the
graph stays connected by construction.

\paragraph{Cohort flow.} The Roney deposit contains $100$ meshes and all $100$
are used. The UW deposit contains $164$ meshes, which are $82$ patients imaged
twice, before and after ablation; the primary analysis uses the $82$
pre-ablation meshes only, since post-ablation geometry encodes the
delivered treatment. No subject is excluded for any other reason, so the analysed
cohort is $100+82=182$ and there is no attrition to report. The $164$-mesh figure
appears in this paper only where a statement concerns the released files
themselves, such as the fibre-field and element-tag audits.

\paragraph{Inducibility protocol.} Labels come from a monodomain
Mitchell--Schaeffer solve on the coarsened surface ($2000$ nodes, vertex
clustering). Membrane parameters, frozen before any label was generated:
$\tau_{\mathrm{in}}=0.3$, $\tau_{\mathrm{out}}=6.0$, $\tau_{\mathrm{open}}=120$,
$\tau_{\mathrm{close}}=110$~ms and $v_{\mathrm{gate}}=0.13$, with fibrosis
shortening $\tau_{\mathrm{close}}$ by up to $50\%$. Diffusion uses an anisotropic
conductance-weighted cotangent Laplacian with $d_0=0.20$ and $w_{\mathrm{ref}}=0.3$,
integrated by backward Euler at $\Delta t=0.05$~ms. Induction is burst pacing from
$2$ randomly chosen sites, $6$ beats at a cycle length of $150$~ms, stimulus
amplitude $0.15$ over $2$~ms within a $4$~mm radius. A subject is labelled
inducible if self-sustained supra-threshold activity persists at least $650$~ms
after the last stimulus, observed over a $1000$~ms window. The protocol was
verified before use: APD90 $218$~ms healthy and $121$~ms fibrotic, planar
conduction velocity $0.4$--$1.2$~m/s, and a monotone relationship between fibrosis
and sustained reentry.

\paragraph{Evaluation pipeline.} Cross-validation is \code{GroupKFold} with the
group set to the patient, so no subject appears in both training and test folds,
using $k=\min(5,\,n_{\mathrm{groups}})$ folds. Of the two classifiers, logistic
regression is a standardisation-plus-\code{LogisticRegression} pipeline
(\code{liblinear}, at most $2000$ iterations) whose regularisation $C$ is chosen
in an inner \code{GroupKFold} over $\{0.03,0.1,0.3,1.0,3.0\}$, so that tuning
never sees the outer test fold, whereas gradient boosting is deliberately
\emph{not} tuned ($120$ trees, depth $2$, learning rate $0.05$, subsample $0.8$)
because at these sample sizes a search would fit the folds; it is a fixed,
shallow reference and not a competitive model. We log it as a deviation from
the pre-registered nested selection, which specifies an inner \code{GroupKFold}
search for both classifiers. Out-of-fold predicted probabilities
are pooled across folds and compared by the DeLong test. Pooling out-of-fold
scores means the two compared vectors come from $k$ different fitted models,
not from the two fixed scoring functions that DeLong's test assumes; the
paired structure and the leakage-free grouping are preserved, but the resulting
$p$-values should be read as approximate. The naive comparison reported alongside
replaces the grouping with shuffled \code{KFold}. On the real cohorts each
patient contributes one row and is its own group, so the grouping constraint is
never binding --- no partition of the rows can place a patient in both training
and test --- and \code{GroupKFold} degenerates into a deterministic split; the
grouping is a guarantee rather than a correction. (It does not reduce to
\emph{unshuffled} \code{KFold}: with equally sized groups \code{scikit-learn}
assigns them round-robin, so the folds are strided rather than contiguous. The
distinction does not affect any reported number, since no unshuffled-\code{KFold}
arm is reported.) The leakage the grouping exists to prevent is demonstrated on
the replicated synthetic cohort, where resamples of a single shape family have
several members (Section~\ref{sec:falsify}).

\paragraph{Substrate.} Real left-atrial surfaces are loaded into weighted
conduction graphs from two public cohorts: the Roney LA virtual cohort (Zenodo
5801337~\cite{roney2022}) and the UW/Boyle LGE-MRI cohort (Dryad
\code{10.5061/dryad.kkwh70sg0}, $82$ distinct AF patients~\cite{uwboyle2025}).
The two encode fibrosis differently, which matters wherever their burdens are
compared. Each Roney mesh ships an image-intensity-ratio (IIR) field, mapped to a
continuous fibrosis fraction by the Khurram healthy-to-scar
ramp~\cite{khurram2014}. The UW release ships no IIR (the intensity ratios are
withheld under that cohort's IRB), so its fibrosis is derived instead from the
per-element tissue tags, which the cohort's authors define as atrial
non-fibrotic, atrial fibrotic, veins and valves, and ablation scar. On the
pre-ablation meshes analysed here only the first three occur, so UW fibrosis is
two-valued once the non-myocardial class is removed, where Roney's is continuous.
The cohorts differ in what else they carry: the Roney meshes ship registered
universal atrial coordinates and per-vertex fibre fields (directional spread
$0.922$--$0.987$), whereas the UW meshes ship no atrial coordinates, for which we
substitute a PCA chart, and no fibre orientations, likewise withheld, so
that the \code{VECTORS fiber} array they do ship is the constant $(1,0,0)$
everywhere (spread $0.000$) and UW anisotropy is a fixed coordinate bias rather
than anatomy (Sections~\ref{sec:regions} and~\ref{sec:substrate}). Meshes
are coarsened to
$\sim\!2000$-node graphs by field-preserving vertex clustering.

\paragraph{Labels.} Ground truth is a monodomain Mitchell--Schaeffer
reaction--diffusion inducibility verdict~\cite{mitchell2003} (self-sustained
reentry $\geq 650$~ms after burst pacing at cycle length $150$~ms from random
sites), run on each real anatomy with an anisotropic conductance-weighted
cotangent Laplacian and implicit time stepping. Reentry here is governed by
wavelength (CV$\times$ERP), source--sink mismatch, and unidirectional block,
\emph{never} by $\lam_2$, so a predictive SFI$\to$label link is not circular.
These verdicts are those of a monodomain simulator and not clinical
post-operative AF. openCARP is run alongside on all $182$ subjects as a
robustness check rather than as the labeller
(\code{results/\allowbreak opencarp\_full\_cohort.json}),
and agrees with the monodomain verdict on $156/182$.

\paragraph{The SFI feature and its competitors.} Per-region SFI is computed as
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:sfi} (first-order, subspace, and exact-recompute variants). It
is evaluated strictly as an add-on/ablation against a non-trivial competitor set
that it must beat: fibrosis burden, fibrosis spatial entropy, fibrosis patch
size, deterministic global min-cut (Stoer--Wagner), percolation threshold, and
$\lam_2$-alone, which isolates what the SFI construction adds over the bare
algebraic connectivity.

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol.} The pre-registered guards are shape-family
\code{GroupKFold} (each patient is its own group, so cross-validation is
patient-held-out, with grouped and naive AUC both reported so that leakage is
visible), the DeLong paired test~\cite{delong1988} in its fast midrank
form~\cite{sunxu2014}, $\geq 10^4$ stratified bootstrap resamples (a logged
deviation: the Kuramoto transfer arm was run at $5\times10^3$), a
rotational/torus-shift spatial null for localization, tie-robust permutation
nulls, and a keep/delete rule deciding each sub-claim by \emph{measured}
correlation. Gating applies to the effect size as well as to the $p$-value: the
primary endpoint requires \emph{both} grouped $\dAUC\geq 0.05$ and DeLong
$p<0.05$.

\paragraph{Synthetic media, generative model.} The synthetic cohorts are random
geometric graphs, which give a two-dimensional excitable sheet with the
degree heterogeneity of real tissue and none of its anatomy. Each network draws
$n$ points uniformly on the unit square and connects every pair within a fixed
radius; if that radius leaves the graph with no edges at all the construction
falls back to a symmetric $5$-nearest-neighbour graph (each node joined to its
five nearest neighbours, mean degree $\approx 6$ after symmetrisation, though at
the radii used this branch is never taken), and in either case only the largest
connected component is retained, so the medium is a single sheet, not
several. Lesion burden is swept uniformly over $[0.05,\,0.45]$ across the cohort
so that both classes appear, the network being stable at the low end and unstable
at the high end, mirroring the mild-to-severe fibrosis gradient of the
atrial cohorts. Because the topology is generated rather than measured, these
cohorts carry no fibre field and no anatomical coordinates, and they are used
only where those are irrelevant: for the scale behaviour of the null and for
the cross-medium transfer of the localizer.

\paragraph{Transfer media.} The identical calculus is applied to
FitzHugh--Nagumo (FHN) excitable networks and Kuramoto phase-oscillator networks
(a different dynamical class), where $\lam_2$ governs synchronizability, to
separate what is a property of the mathematics from an accident of one dataset.

The negative is not uniform across regimes. On the synthetic cohorts (up to
$100{,}000$ networks) the null is clean, the effect converging to
$\dAUC\approx+0.003$ and becoming statistically resolvable only because $N$ is
enormous, whereas on the real cohorts it is a measured null: the combined
gradient-boosting point estimate is $+0.012$ (DeLong $p=0.449$, bootstrap CI
$[-0.017,+0.044]$), roughly a quarter of the pre-registered $0.05$ threshold and
with an interval that excludes it. An earlier version of this analysis reported
$+0.051$ here and had to argue that the endpoint was missed only on
significance. That reading does not survive, and the two changes behind its
collapse weigh unequally. Correcting the sealed UW substrate while holding the
cohort at its original size takes the estimate to $+0.036$ --- already below the
pre-registered gate, but with most of the old figure intact, so it was not
principally a substrate artefact; extending the Roney arm to all $100$ released
meshes then takes it to $+0.012$. The larger share of the drop is sample size
rather than substrate. The provenance audit that found the substrate misreading
is Section~\ref{sec:substrate}, and the correction is set out alongside the other
two in Section~\ref{sec:corrections}. With $42$
inducible of $182$ this is the largest real-anatomy arm in the study, and the
null here is a measurement and not a shortfall. The combined row is a single
pooled ROC across two cohorts of unequal prevalence ($34/100$ on Roney, $8/82$
on UW), so its ranking carries between-cohort as well as within-cohort
separability; the pooled base AUC under logistic regression, $0.884$,
consequently exceeds both components ($0.870$ and $0.834$), and the pooled
increment should be read alongside the cohort-specific estimates, which it does
not replace.

The UW cohort remains the weak arm, though not for the reason we previously
gave. On eight positives its intervals are wide, the competitor comparison
spanning $[-0.095,+0.203]$ under gradient boosting, so it is an underpowered
external check of the labeller rather than of SFI. We had also described its
competitor baseline as close to chance, and that was simply wrong: the measured
competitor base AUC is $0.834$ under logistic regression and $0.748$ under
gradient boosting. Only the \emph{fibrosis-only} baseline approaches chance, at
$0.562$, and it is that near-chance baseline which manufactures the cohort's
largest apparent effect, $\dAUC=+0.217$ with $p=0.121$ and an interval
$[-0.030,+0.485]$ that spans half the available range; a large ratio over a
baseline that predicts almost nothing, on eight events, is not a signal.

The null is not explained by the validity radius: the \emph{exact} per-region
$\Delta\lam_2$ SFI, which carries no first-order approximation error and is
immune to the $\rhoval$ argument, also adds nothing over the competitor set. Our
reading is that SFI's information is already present in standard connectivity
features ($\lam_2$-alone, min-cut, percolation), but an incremental null does
not by itself separate that redundancy from a feature that carries little
predictive information at all. We separate the two in
Section~\ref{sec:redundancy}, directly and without reference to the label, where
the redundancy account holds for the region-level aggregates and fails for the
extreme and edge-level columns. Either way the incremental null is itself a
label-dependent finding, separate from $\rhoval$.

\paragraph{Fibrosis-heterogeneity baseline comparison, and its withdrawal.}
This comparison is not the pre-registered secondary endpoint, which is the
localization test reported in Section~\ref{sec:localize}, but a further
feature-set contrast pre-specified alongside the primary one. An earlier version
of this analysis reported that SFI beat a fibrosis-heterogeneity-only baseline
on the combined real cohort (GBT $\dAUC=+0.103$, $p=0.009$) and read that as
evidence that SFI carries connectivity information beyond the substrate rather
than re-encoding fibrosis. That result does not survive, and we withdraw both it
and the claim it supported. Two changes separate the old number from the new
one, and they weigh unequally; both figures below are gradient-boosting $\dAUC$
against the same fibrosis-heterogeneity baseline, tested with the paired DeLong
statistic. Correcting the UW substrate while holding the cohort at its original
$144$ subjects leaves the estimate largely intact ($+0.093$, $p=0.013$), so the
old result was not principally a substrate artefact; it collapses instead when
the Roney arm is extended from $62$ to the full $100$ released meshes, to
$\dAUC=+0.017$ ($p=0.452$). An effect that disappears when $38$ further real
subjects are added was a small-sample fluctuation, and it was one of
$\sim\!30$ DeLong comparisons besides. On the full cohort no comparison,
primary or secondary, shows SFI adding information beyond either baseline.

The failure mode of the discarded estimate is worth recording. Under the sealed
substrate the UW arm alone returned $\dAUC=+0.340$ ($p=0.026$) on six positives,
a value no one should have believed and which we did not flag at the time, and
it was that arm which carried the combined figure; a large point estimate
resting on six events is a warning, not a finding.

\paragraph{Naive (ungrouped) comparison.} The pre-registration asks for the naive
$\dAUC$ beside the grouped one so that leakage is visible, and it is reported
here for every real-cohort arm: Roney $+0.017$ (lr) and $-0.011$ (gbt), UW
$+0.066$ and $-0.160$, combined $-0.011$ and $+0.010$, against the grouped
values of Table~\ref{tab:null}. The gap has no consistent sign: the naive
estimate runs above the grouped one in two of the six arms, both logistic
regression on a single cohort, and below it in the other four including the
pooled logistic arm, so nothing here reproduces the optimism ungrouped
cross-validation is expected to show. That is expected and offers no reassurance:
as the Methods note, patient-level grouping is close to a no-op on real anatomy,
so these six numbers are not a leakage demonstration, and the demonstration
instead rests on the replicated synthetic cohort, where resampled networks share
a parent shape family.

\paragraph{Power analysis.} In the synthetic cohort the subspace effect
($+0.0028$) needs $\sim\!4{,}150$ cases for $80\%$ power and the single-vector
effect ($+0.0007$) $\sim\!23{,}000$, clinically undetectable \emph{there}. We do
\emph{not} transport ``clinically undetectable'' to the real cohorts, which are a
separate regime. On the corrected substrate their estimates are $+0.009$ (Roney,
$n=100$) and $+0.012$ (combined, $n=182$), with intervals that contain zero and
exclude the $0.05$ gate. An earlier version read the then-current
$+0.036$--$0.051$ as compatible with a detectable effect we were underpowered to
confirm; those estimates are superseded and that reading no longer holds.

\subsection{The clinical endpoint, and the false positive its own protocol caught}
\label{sec:clinical}
\noindent\emph{Source:}
\code{results/\allowbreak clinical\allowbreak\_endpoint.json}.

Every endpoint above is scored against a simulator's verdict, whereas this one is
scored against patient outcomes. The cohort's authors supplied per-patient
two-year post-ablation outcomes for all $82$ UW patients (recurrence being at
least $30$~s of documented atrial fibrillation or flutter after a $90$-day
blanking period, with every patient followed the full two years), and \S8 of the
pre-registration, which governs this analysis, was frozen and git-tagged before
any feature was placed beside that column. The analysis was run once, and its
result is reported as it came out.

Three commitments in that protocol, all of them predating the data join, govern
how the result should be read. First, the $0.05$ effect-size gate used for the
simulator endpoint is \emph{not} reused here: at $n=82$ with $48$ events, power at
$\dAUC=0.05$ is $0.24$, so that gate would be unfalsifiable, and the minimum
detectable effect was fixed at $\dAUC\approx0.10$--$0.15$. Second, testing follows
a fixed sequence and halts at the first non-rejection. Third, and decisively here,
\S8.6 anticipated that UW fibrosis is categorical, not continuous, and warned
in writing that this ``biases the comparison toward SFI, because a degraded
baseline is easier to beat,'' and pre-committed that if our baseline came out
materially weaker than the source study's published performance, any $\dAUC$ would
be reported as \emph{confounded by baseline degradation} and not as a win for
SFI.

Under logistic regression the competitor baseline gives an out-of-fold AUC of
$0.250$, adding SFI gives $0.447$, and the increment is $\dAUC=+0.197$ with a
bootstrap interval $[+0.054,+0.334]$ and a paired DeLong $p=0.0061$, clearing both
pre-registered thresholds on real patients with a $p$-value an order of magnitude
below $0.05$.

The increment is nonetheless an artefact, and the pre-registered protocol
classified it as one before we reached that judgement ourselves. A baseline AUC of
$0.250$ is anti-predictive, not weak; adding SFI reaches only $0.447$, still
below chance. Three checks confirm the reading: every one of the fifteen
features, competitor and SFI alike, is univariately at chance on this outcome
($0.435$--$0.547$); the gradient-boosting arm, whose baseline sits at chance
($0.516$), gives $\dAUC=+0.021$ at $p=0.738$; and when the nine SFI columns are
replaced by nine columns of standard Gaussian noise and the identical pipeline is
re-run, the increment reproduces, the noise gaining $+0.137$ on average across
twenty draws, with $6$ of $20$ matching or exceeding SFI's $+0.197$ (empirical
$p=0.333$). The logistic arm therefore measures dilution instead of prediction:
padding an over-fitted, anti-predictive baseline with any nine columns pulls its
AUC back toward $0.5$.

The inversion itself follows from a property of the estimator rather than from
anything about these patients. The folds carry very unequal event rates ($25\%$ to
$81\%$), and because each training set is what remains once its test fold is
removed, the two prevalences move in opposition. The model calibrates to its
\emph{training}-fold prevalence: the mean predicted probability on a held-out fold
tracks that fold's \emph{training} prevalence at $r=+0.995$, so the fold with
fewest events receives the highest predicted probabilities. Pooling out-of-fold probabilities across such folds inverts the
ranking. Scoring within folds and averaging removes most of it ($0.250\to0.345$
for the baseline), which is a diagnostic and not a re-analysis.

The clinical primary endpoint is \textbf{not met}, and per \S8.6 the observed
increment is reported as confounded by baseline degradation. Under the \S8.7
stopping rule the ablation-induced $\Delta$SFI test is therefore not performed; it
was not computed, since running it and then declining to report it would spend the
very $\alpha$ the sequence protects. Two analyses outside the confirmatory family
are reported for completeness: on the data provider's own $67/15$ split
(explicitly \emph{not} independent, since those $15$ patients also enter the
primary, and not powered, with $8$ events) the two classifiers disagree in sign
($+0.196$ and $-0.107$); and among the $48$ recurrers, SFI does not separate the
$35$ who presented with fibrillation from the $13$ who presented with flutter
($\dAUC=-0.009$ and $-0.044$), the expected direction given that flutter is
typically macro-reentrant and often right-atrial.

We report this at length because it is the paper's thesis instantiated on real
clinical outcomes rather than on simulated ones. A naive analysis (a $p<0.05$
threshold, no effect-size gate, no baseline-quality check, no noise control) would
have published $\dAUC=+0.197$ at $p=0.0061$ as evidence that a spectral biomarker
predicts atrial-fibrillation recurrence in patients. The guard that caught it was
written into \S8.6 on the day the data arrived, before any feature met the outcome
column, by authors who expected to be the ones fooled.

\subsection{Testing the redundancy explanation directly}
\label{sec:redundancy}
\noindent\emph{Source:}
\code{results/\allowbreak feature\allowbreak\_redundancy.json}.

The account above attributes the null to feature redundancy, an explanation inferred
from the incremental AUC being zero. A zero increment does not establish it, since a
feature that is pure noise also adds nothing, as does one the classifier cannot
exploit, and those are three different diagnoses. We therefore measured redundancy
directly and without reference to the label, by regressing each SFI column on the
competitor block and by taking the leading canonical correlation between the two
blocks, with a permuted-SFI control to separate genuine redundancy from a degenerate
estimator.

The blocks are almost collinear in their dominant direction: the leading canonical
correlation between the nine SFI columns and the six competitors is $0.996$, against
$0.289$ for the permuted control. Taken alone that supports the redundancy account,
but the per-column picture is less uniform:

\small

Four of the nine columns are reconstructible from the competitors at
$R^2\geq0.60$, and the two aggregate summaries almost perfectly so. The remaining
five keep most of their variance, at $R^2<0.25$, well above the permuted control
but far from redundant. The split does not fall along the aggregate versus
order-statistic line: the competitors reconstruct the two region-level aggregates
together with the deeper region order statistics \code{sfi\_top2} and
\code{sfi\_top3}, while the edge-level pair, the region maximum and the region
spread all resist reconstruction.

This qualifies the null rather than overturning it, and sharpens what the null
means. For \code{sfi\_total} and \code{sfi\_region\_mean} the competitors already
carry the information, and adding them cannot help. For \code{sfi\_region\_max},
\code{sfi\_top1} and \code{sfi\_region\_std} there is genuine unique variance in
the feature, and the finding is the stronger one: that variance exists and does
not predict the label. A biomarker can fail either by saying nothing new or by
saying something new that does not matter, and on this evidence SFI does both in
different components, of which we had previously reported only the first.


\subsection{Region definition and the patient-level reduction}
\label{sec:regions}

The per-region SFI of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sfi} is stated over a region $R$, and until
now we had left $R$ implicit. Since the partition and the reduction that follows
it produce the feature block whose incremental $\dAUC$ is the primary endpoint,
both are given explicitly here. Neither was pre-registered (the pre-registration
refers to the per-region SFI throughout without fixing a partition or a
patient-level reduction), so both are a logged deviation, fixed post hoc but
without reference to the outcome, since SFI is closed-form given the mesh and is
never fitted to the label.

A further deviation bears directly on the validity argument: every real-cohort
number in this paper is computed from the first-order per-region SFI
(\code{asb.sfi.sfi\_region}). The pre-registration also specifies a
gap-triggered switch to the subspace variant when the validity radius is
exceeded, and that switch is not wired into the real-cohort feature path; the
subspace estimator is used only in the synthetic validity experiments of
Section~\ref{sec:validity}. Since real atria sit at median
$\rhoval\approx2422$, the switch would have fired on every mesh had it been
applied. We report the first-order numbers rather than silently re-deriving the
table, since the ranking a classifier consumes is invariant to the global
$\Delta w$ magnitude that drives $\rhoval$, and since the question the switch
exists to answer is settled directly in Section~\ref{sec:null}, where the
\emph{exact} per-region $\Delta\lam_2$, which carries no first-order error at
any $\lVert\Delta L\rVert$, also adds nothing over the competitor set. The
reader should nonetheless know that the primary endpoint was scored with the
estimator that the validity argument indicts.

\paragraph{Partition.} Regions are a $3\times3$ grid over the universal atrial
coordinates. With $(\alpha,\beta)\in[0,1]^2$ per vertex, a vertex is assigned
$r=3\lfloor 3\beta\rfloor+\lfloor 3\alpha\rfloor \in\{0,\dots,8\}$, so every mesh
carries exactly nine regions regardless of its vertex count. An edge is
assigned region $r$ only when both endpoints carry $r$; edges straddling two
regions go to a tenth bucket, $r=-1$, which is retained rather than discarded so
that the per-edge sensitivities sum to the global total. The rule is shared by
the closed-form and Monte-Carlo estimators so their region keys coincide.

At this step the two cohorts differ in kind and not only in degree: the Roney
meshes carry anatomically registered universal atrial coordinates, so their nine
regions are anatomical, whereas the UW release ships none, and we substitute the
top two principal axes of the surface coordinates, min-max normalised. That
surrogate is a smooth two-dimensional chart adequate for binning, but it is not
registered to anatomy, and any region-level or localization claim on the UW
cohort is correspondingly weaker. The substitution is recorded in the mesh
metadata.

\paragraph{Reduction to the patient.} The classification target is one binary
verdict per patient, so the nine per-region values must be reduced. Nine scalars
enter the design matrix, all in the \code{sfi\_} namespace: the global
\code{total} (the sum of expected per-edge $\lam_2$ drop over all edges);
\code{region\_max}, \code{region\_mean} and \code{region\_std} over the region
values; the three largest region values in descending order,
\code{top1}--\code{top3}; and \code{edge\_frag\_max} and \code{edge\_frag\_mean}
over the raw per-edge fragilities $(\phii_{2,i}-\phii_{2,j})^2$. The order
statistics are included because a fragility biomarker is a claim about the
\emph{worst} region rather than the average one, and reporting only a mean would
have made the hypothesis untestable in its own terms. Two of the nine names
denote one quantity: \code{region\_max} and \code{top1} are both the maximum over
the region values, and are identical on all $182$ subjects to zero difference, so
the block carries eight distinct columns under nine names and the two share a row
of the table in Section~\ref{sec:redundancy}.

Because the competitor features are patient-level scalars already, no analogous
reduction is applied to them, and the comparison is between an add-on of eight
distinct columns and a six-column baseline, which if anything favours the add-on.
Nor is any of the nine columns fitted to the outcome: SFI is closed-form given
the mesh and the frozen uncoupling field, so it contributes no tuning degrees of
freedom, and the null cannot be attributed to a failure of feature selection.

\subsection{A provenance audit of the released substrates, and four controls}
\label{sec:substrate}
\noindent\emph{Sources}, all under \code{results/}: the cohort audit is
\code{cohort\allowbreak\_geometry\allowbreak\_audit.json}, and the four controls are
\code{uw\allowbreak\_substrate\allowbreak\_ablation.json},
\code{roney\allowbreak\_fibre\allowbreak\_control.json},
\code{roney\allowbreak\_fibrosis\allowbreak\_quantization.json} and
\code{fibrosis\allowbreak\_burden\allowbreak\_swap.json}. Every Roney inducibility
arm in this section runs on the $62$-mesh subset that predates the extension of
the predictive cohort to all $100$ released meshes, and was not re-run on the
full deposit; the predictive results of Section~\ref{sec:null} use all $100$.

This section is prompted by a discrepancy between the two cohorts under a
protocol frozen before any label was generated and identical in every parameter:
the Roney meshes returned $20/62$ subjects inducible ($32.3\%$) while the UW
meshes returned $6/82$ ($7.3\%$) on the substrate as released. Three UW rates
recur below and are kept distinct throughout: $6/82$ as released, $8/82$ after
the cap repair (the substrate every predictive result uses), and $14/82$ after
both repairs. A fourfold discrepancy on cohorts we had assumed carried
comparable fibrosis burden, an assumption Control~4 below overturns, should be
treated as a hypothesis about the pipeline rather than accepted as a property of
the cohorts. We had previously recorded the UW arm as uninformative and moved
on, and that was the error. The audit is set out first, followed by three
controls that do not explain the anomaly and a fourth that does.

Two problems sit between the release and our use of it, and neither is a defect
in the data; both follow from an undocumented release being read by someone who
guessed. The first guess is ours: the only per-element scalar the UW release
carries is \code{elemTag}, the Dryad README does not define its values, and we
had been mapping tag $164$ to half-fibrotic tissue. Four measurements show that
it is not tissue at all. It resolves into four to six large connected components
in every one of the $82$ meshes, five in $75$ of them, four in five and six in
two, which is the ordinary variation of pulmonary-vein anatomy: a left common
trunk gives four, a right middle vein gives six. Each large component is
topologically a disc, $11$--$42$ mm across, sitting at $0.47$--$0.82$ of the
atrial radius from the centroid. It borders fibrotic elements on about $0.08\%$
of its incident edges against a chance rate of $14$--$20\%$, a depletion beyond
a hundredfold that is incompatible with reading it as a fibrosis border zone. It
is also ablation-invariant, holding at $20.0\%$ of elements before the procedure
and $19.9\%$ after, while every other class moves. Tag $164$ is the set of caps
sealing the pulmonary veins and the mitral valve. Removing it opens the surface:
on $80$ of the $82$ meshes the Euler characteristic falls, by a median of $7$
(range $2$--$12$). We report the \emph{change}, not an opening count,
because $\chi=2-k$ presumes a clean closed surface and these do not start as
one: $\chi$ before removal spans $-18$ to $5$. The two exceptions differ in the
direction of the change, not only in their starting value: on ID040 removal
takes $\chi$
from $-16$ to $-8$ and on ID049 from $-18$ to $-5$, so the full-cohort range
of the change is $-13$ to $12$
(\code{results/\allowbreak uw\allowbreak\_euler\allowbreak\_census.json}).
We state the split because the census in this paragraph replaces an earlier
claim generalised from one mesh, and quoting the $80$-mesh figure over $82$
would repeat that error in the correction itself.

Treating those caps as half-conducting tissue inflates fibrosis, and it also
seals the atrium's orifices, so that simulated activation crosses the mitral
valve and the vein ostia instead of circling them and reentry anchored on those
orifices, a principal mechanism of atrial fibrillation, cannot form.

That identification was made from geometry alone, before any legend for the tags
existed, and has since been confirmed by the cohort's authors, who define $164$
as veins and valves and treat them, as we now do, as electrically dead. The same
correspondence settles a tag this study never encounters: $199$ is ablation scar
and is likewise dead, where our mapping had it as maximally fibrotic \emph{but
still conducting}. It occurs only in the post-ablation meshes and a census
confirms it is absent from all $82$ pre-ablation meshes, so no result here is
affected; the code now refuses to load any mesh carrying a dead tag it has not
been told to remove, so the assumption cannot persist unchecked. We record the
confirmation because a census that agrees with the ground truth is worth more as
evidence than the same census standing alone, and because the audit below rests
on it.

The second problem is upstream, and it is a placeholder mistaken for data. A
\code{VECTORS fiber} array is present in all
$164$ released meshes, but it equals the constant $(1,0,0)$: the measured mean
directional spread is exactly $0.000$. The Roney cohort, by contrast, spans
$0.922$--$0.987$ across its $62$ meshes (mean $0.954$), with roughly one
distinct direction per vertex. Conduction anisotropy is applied relative to the
local fibre direction, so a single global direction reduces it to a fixed
coordinate bias carrying no anatomy. The same cardinality argument applies to
fibrosis: three distinct levels in the UW release, effectively two once the caps
are dropped, against $700$--$1646$ (mean $1318$) in Roney at the same
$2000$-node coarsening.

Here too the cohort's authors have since supplied the explanation, and it is the
benign one: fibre orientations are withheld under the same IRB as the intensity
ratios, and are meant to be regenerated with published tools, not read off the
release. The constant array is a placeholder, and nothing is wrong with the
deposit; what was wrong was our reading it as anatomy, the same failure as the
tag mapping and with the same root in an undocumented file used without asking.
Both were caught by measurement rather than by correspondence (the degeneracy is
visible in the array itself), but neither would have occurred had we asked
first.

\paragraph{Control 1: repairing the substrate.} Opening the orifices and
supplying a spatially varying fibre field, in a $2\times2$ design over all $82$
subjects (Fig.~\ref{fig:substrate}a), raises inducibility from $6/82$ to
$14/82$. Either repair alone converts two subjects ($6\to8$ in both single-factor arms)
and together they convert eight, so on these four cells the averaged main effect
of a repair and the interaction contrast are both $+4$ subjects and cannot be
told apart; we do not claim the two act super-additively. The mechanistic reading
an interaction would invite (that reentry needs both an anatomical obstacle and
anisotropic heterogeneity) is in any case undercut by Control~2 below, which
shows the fibre factor does not move the rate at all on a cohort that has a real
fibre field. We do not present this as a fix: the strongest contrast does not
reach paired significance (McNemar exact $p=0.077$), it closes only $39\%$ of
the distance to Roney, and only two of the original six positives survive the
repair. The varying fibre field is itself a probe rather than a correction: it
is the global $+z$ direction projected onto each vertex tangent plane, and it is
not anatomy.

\paragraph{Control 2: destroying the fibres on a cohort that has them.} The
cleaner experiment replaces the Roney fibre field with the same constant
$(1,0,0)$, holding anatomy, fibrosis and every solver parameter fixed
(Fig.~\ref{fig:substrate}b). The rate does not move at all: $20/62$ with real
fibres and $20/62$ without, McNemar $b=c=5$, $p=1.0$. Fibre degeneracy does not
drive the inducibility rate, and our first attribution of the UW anomaly to it
was wrong. The perturbation is real at the level of the dynamics (sustained
reentry duration changes on $50/62$ subjects, by up to $661$ ms, and $10/62$
verdicts flip), but those effects cancel in aggregate.

\paragraph{Control 3: destroying the fibrosis gradation.} The remaining
structural difference was that UW fibrosis is binary where Roney's is graded, and
graded border zones are where slow conduction and unidirectional block are
supposed to arise. Binarising the Roney fibrosis field should therefore have
suppressed reentry, and it does the opposite (Fig.~\ref{fig:substrate}c).
Thresholding at $f>0.5$ raises inducibility from $20/62$ to $31/62$; thresholding
at the per-subject quantile that holds mean fibrosis burden fixed at $0.333$, so
that only the gradation is destroyed, raises it to $40/62$. Coarsening the
fibrosis field roughly doubles inducibility. In hindsight the mechanism is
unmysterious (a sharp binary boundary is a stronger source--sink discontinuity
than a smooth ramp), but the direction is the opposite of our prediction, and it
disqualifies coarse fibrosis as an explanation for a cohort that is inducible
\emph{too rarely}.

Taken together the three substrate controls leave the anomaly unexplained, and
they tighten rather than loosen it: of the two defects we can manipulate, one
does not move the rate at all and the other, imposed deliberately, moves it
upward. None of the three establishes an effect, which is weaker than
excluding one, and none of them points at a substrate defect as the cause. The
search accordingly moved from what might be broken in these meshes to what they
contain.

\paragraph{Control 4: the cohorts do not carry comparable fibrosis.} Having
exhausted the defects, we measured what the solver is given. Coarsened to the
same $2000$ nodes, mean fibrosis over the meshes these controls simulate is
$0.333$ on Roney's $62$ against $0.248$ on UW's $82$: the UW cohort carries
about $25\%$ less. (A separate $40$-mesh-per-cohort geometry audit gives $0.349$
against $0.244$, with the fraction of tissue above half-fibrotic at $0.310$
against $0.239$; we quote the simulated meshes here because they produced the
rates.) That deficit is measured on the cap-repaired substrate, which matters
for reading the history. As released, with the caps still counted as
half-fibrotic, UW measured $0.307$ against Roney's $0.333$ (only about $8\%$
lower), so the cap defect was itself masking most of the burden difference, and
the $6/82$ that opened this section was produced under that masking. Every
predictive result in the paper uses the repaired substrate, where the deficit is
the full $25\%$. A second qualification limits how the deficit should be read:
the two burdens are not measured the same way. Roney's is the mean of a
continuous IIR-derived ramp; UW's, whose intensity ratios are withheld, is the
mean of a two-valued tag classification, so it is closer to a fibrotic-area
fraction than to a mean severity. Both enter the solver through the identical
conductance ramp, so the comparison is operationally sound (the solver sees the
same kind of field either way), but $25\%$ is a difference between two
differently constructed quantities and should not be read as a claim that these
patients carry a quarter less fibrosis in any imaging sense. What does not
depend on the comparison is the swap below, which rescales each cohort's own
field and is therefore internal to it. The frozen protocol's own calibration
reports a rank correlation of $0.75$ between fibrosis and sustained reentry, so
a cohort with less of it should be less inducible, and the lower rate is
therefore the labeller behaving as specified rather than an anomaly.

Swapping the burden between cohorts confirms it in both directions
(Fig.~\ref{fig:substrate}d). Rescaling Roney's fibrosis multiplicatively to UW's
level, which preserves the spatial pattern and the gradation and moves only the
level, collapses inducibility from $20/62$ to $1/62$ (Fisher exact
$p=4.0\times10^{-6}$). Rescaling UW's upward toward Roney's raises it from
$8/82$ to $21/82$ ($p=0.013$). Both control arms reproduce their native rates
exactly. The effect is large, symmetric, and an order of magnitude better
supported than any of the three substrate hypotheses.

At matched burden the point estimates invert, which bears on the original
question: Roney rescaled to $0.241$ gives $1/62$ while UW at $0.248$ gives
$8/82$. On one and eight events that contrast does not establish a reversal and
we do not claim one; it shows only that the UW cohort is not intrinsically
resistant to reentry. Most of the apparent anomaly is accounted for by a
difference in how much fibrosis the two released datasets contain, and the two
released defects, real as they are, were never the explanation; the burden
account does not cover openCARP's UW rate, which the same rescaling leaves at
$0/30$ (Section~\ref{sec:opencarp}). We had spent three controls looking for
something broken before checking whether the two cohorts were comparable in the
first place: an out-of-range aggregate is a hypothesis about the pipeline, but a
hypothesis about the inputs first.

\subsection{An independent solver as a robustness check}
\label{sec:opencarp}
\noindent\emph{Sources}, all under \code{results/}:
\code{opencarp\allowbreak\_full\allowbreak\_cohort.json}, with the
per-cohort and chance-corrected concordance in
\code{opencarp\allowbreak\_concordance\allowbreak\_detail.json} and the endpoint
re-scored against openCARP's verdicts in
\code{gm1\allowbreak\_opencarp\allowbreak\_labels.json};
all three calibration attempts, including the two rejected, are in
\code{opencarp\allowbreak\_calibration\allowbreak\_history.json},
with the discretisation and propagation checks in
\code{opencarp\allowbreak\_bin\allowbreak\_sensitivity.json} and
\code{opencarp\allowbreak\_uw\allowbreak\_propagation.json}.

Every in-silico result above rests on one phenomenological labeller, a monodomain
Mitchell--Schaeffer solver substituted for the pre-registered openCARP ground
truth when openCARP could not be installed. That substitution is now testable,
because openCARP runs. Replacing the labeller with it would invalidate every
number at once, so we instead run both solvers and report their agreement.

\paragraph{Calibration.} openCARP ships the same Mitchell--Schaeffer membrane
model, so the comparison is between solvers rather than between physics, but its
defaults are not ours. Two quantities were matched to the frozen configuration,
action-potential duration and conduction velocity, each measured independently of
the endpoint. For action-potential duration, openCARP's
default \code{tau\_close} $150$ gives APD90 $=277$~ms, against the frozen
$218$~ms healthy and $121$~ms fibrotic; measured on this build, \code{tau\_close}
$110\to209$~ms and $55\to113$~ms, so $115\times(1-0.5f)$ reproduces both within
about $2\%$. For conduction velocity, the shipped conductivity gives $0.344$~m/s
against the frozen band of $0.4$--$1.2$~m/s. That is a wavelength of roughly
$75$~mm in an atrium of $\sim\!110$~mm characteristic length, and it makes reentry
far too easy. Raising the intracellular conductivity alone saturates, because the
monodomain conductivity is the harmonic mean of the intra- and extracellular
values; scaling both gives $0.852$~m/s at $g_{il}=1.00$, and we adopt $1.05$.

Two configurations were rejected before this one, at $90\%$ and $76.7\%$
inducible with \emph{negative} fibrosis--verdict correlations, the signature of
rate-dependent block and not of reentry. We record that because it is the
protocol working: no endpoint parameter was adjusted at any stage, so the
calibration gate remained able to fail, and on those two configurations it did.

On the calibrated configuration the pre-registered gate is met on Roney but not
on UW. Roney gives $22/100$ inducible ($22.0\%$, inside the $10$--$40\%$ band)
with a fibrosis--verdict rank correlation of $+0.404$, against the frozen
solver's $0.75$, whereas on UW openCARP returns $4/82$ inducible ($4.9\%$, below
the band) with a rank correlation of only $+0.079$.
Across all $182$ subjects the two solvers agree on $156$, or $85.7\%$. That figure
is raw and has not been corrected for chance. On an outcome this imbalanced the
correction matters: Cohen's $\kappa$ is $0.536$, and the two solvers agree on
only $21$ positives. The cohort-level split shows why the pooled number should
not be read as a tissue-level result. On Roney, where the gate is met, raw
agreement is $82/100$ ($82.0\%$) at $\kappa=0.561$; on UW, where it is not, raw
agreement is higher at $74/82$ ($90.2\%$) but $\kappa$ falls to $0.287$,
because at a $4.9\%$ and $9.8\%$ event rate almost all of that agreement is
shared negatives. The pooled figure is therefore a statement about the protocol
across both cohorts, not about the tissue, and the arm that looks most
concordant is the one carrying the least information. The substantive part is
the asymmetry: of the $26$ subjects the two solvers disagree
on, openCARP calls only $5$ inducible where the monodomain solver does not, while
the reverse happens $21$ times; over the whole cohort openCARP labels $26/182$
against monodomain's $42/182$. Sampling noise does not explain that imbalance
(McNemar exact $p=0.0025$); it is systematic, so the two solvers differ in level
and not only in which subjects they pick. Relative to an independent reaction--diffusion
solver, then, the stand-in is the more liberal of the two and does not
under-call, so the incremental comparisons reported above are conservative
rather than inflated. (On the $60$-subject calibration subset the discordance was
entirely one-way, $0$ against $5$; that does not hold on the full cohort and the
figure quoted here is the full-cohort one.)

\paragraph{The comparison re-scored against openCARP (exploratory).} Agreement on
the label does not by itself bound the incremental $\dAUC$, so we ran the
comparison instead of inferring it: the identical GM1 evaluation, on identical
features, with the outcome column swapped from the monodomain verdict to
openCARP's
(\code{results/\allowbreak gm1\allowbreak\_opencarp\allowbreak\_labels.json}).
This is \emph{not} an endpoint and cannot become one. The pre-registration binds
openCARP labels out of any endpoint until the calibration gate is re-passed on
that solver, and the gate failed on UW; the arm is reported as an exploratory
sensitivity analysis under that precondition, and no gate verdict
is claimed from it. The null holds: the combined competitor-baseline increment is $\dAUC=+0.028$
($p=0.328$), against $+0.012$ under the stand-in; per cohort it is $+0.033$ on
Roney ($p=0.211$) and $+0.067$ on UW ($p=0.445$), and no arm in either family
clears the pre-registered $0.05$ gate at $p<0.05$. This arm is less
powered by construction, since openCARP calls $26$ subjects inducible against the
stand-in's $42$ and UW contributes only $4$, so the intervals are wider and
the combined one, $[-0.027,+0.085]$, no longer excludes the gate the way the
stand-in's does; the UW arm here also sits on the cohort whose calibration gate
failed. The result is therefore that the null is not an artefact of the
labeller, not that openCARP independently establishes it.

The check is bounded in two ways. The
fibrosis field must be discretised into conductivity classes for openCARP, and the
verdict is unstable at five classes ($1/20$) though identical at ten, twenty and
forty ($7/20$, with complete per-subject agreement), so the operating choice of
twenty sits inside the converged regime while the coarse limit is not benign. The
larger bound is openCARP's $4/82$ on the UW cohort, which unlike the monodomain
solver is \emph{not} recovered by raising that cohort's fibrosis burden:
rescaling UW upward leaves the rate at $0/30$. The mundane explanations do not
survive: propagation failure is ruled out, because on UW meshes the wave reaches
$99.4\%$ of nodes and peak depolarisation averages $0.83$, so activation spreads
across the surface and then dies; it is not confined to the stimulus site.
The coarser discretisation is ruled out as well: at the same node budget the
$40$-mesh-per-cohort geometry audit gives UW a mean edge of $3.12$~mm against
Roney's $2.91$~mm, but the convergence
study shows coarser meshes \emph{over}-call ($58.3\%$ at $1500$ nodes), so that
difference predicts a higher UW rate, not a near-zero one.

What remains is a genuine substrate difference that the burden rescaling does not
capture, and we do not know what it is. The monodomain burden result
(Section~\ref{sec:substrate}) is established in both directions on that solver and
is unaffected by this; what is not established is that openCARP's near-zero UW rate
has the same cause. We report the discrepancy rather than resolve it, and restrict
the concordance claim to the cohort where both solvers are calibrated. A
robustness check that agreed everywhere would be less informative than one that
disagrees somewhere and reports the disagreement.

\subsection{The labeller is reproducible in aggregate and unstable per subject}
\label{sec:labelnoise}

The controls expose a property of the ground truth that matters more than the
substrate defects do. Destroying the Roney fibre field holds the aggregate rate
at exactly $20/62$ while flipping $10/62$ individual verdicts; repairing the UW
substrate retains only two of its six original positives; and the six-tier
convergence study reported below finds only $7/24$ subjects consistent across
resolutions. The aggregate inducibility rate is therefore a stable quantity,
while the per-subject verdict is not.

The consequence falls on the endpoint itself: the pre-registered comparison is
scored on per-subject labels, and non-differential noise in a binary outcome
attenuates any incremental AUC toward zero. Part of the null we report is thus a
property of the labeller rather than of the biomarker, and we cannot cleanly
separate the two with the present ground truth. The same noise sets a floor on
what any feature could achieve here, a limit that applies to the whole class of
studies scoring spectral or structural features against a phenomenological
inducibility verdict rather than to SFI alone. The pre-registered openCARP
ground truth, substituted here for a monodomain Mitchell--Schaeffer solver,
would need to be run before the residual is attributable.

\subsection{The validity radius: why the single-vector estimator fails}
The relative error of the first-order SFI scales as $\Theta(\rhoval)$ and crosses
$10\%$ at $\rhoval^\ast\approx3$, while real atria sit at median
$\rhoval\approx2422$. This proves the single-vector estimator is numerically
invalid on real tissue and $\phii_2$ is ill-conditioned, but (as stated in
Section~\ref{sec:validity}) it does not by itself prove non-predictiveness; the
predictive null of Section~\ref{sec:null} is a separate, label-dependent
finding, and Section~\ref{sec:redundancy} shows redundancy accounts for only
part of it.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$\rhoval$}{rho} scaling law}
\label{sec:scaling}
The gap collapses with system size by Weyl's law~\cite{weyl1912}: for a
$d$-dimensional domain the low Laplacian eigenvalues scale as $\lam_k\sim(k/N)^{2/d}$,
so $\rhoval$ grows lawfully with resolution.

\subsection{Localization transfers across three media (weakly)}
\label{sec:localize}
The \emph{strict} pre-registered localization endpoint is null for every field,
including $\lvert\nabla\phii_2\rvert$: its median \emph{argmax-geodesic error}
---the shortest-path distance along the surface from the localizer's argmax to
the simulator's reentry origin, expressed as a fraction of the mesh's maximum
geodesic extent rather than in millimetres---is
$0.472$ of the maximum geodesic extent ($48.3$~mm), with a bootstrap $95\%$ CI
of $[0.350,0.656]$, and it is \emph{not} below the spatial-null 5th percentile
$0.406$ (rotational/torus-shift permutation $p=0.229$). Because the interval
straddles that threshold, on the $20$ localized subjects the endpoint is neither
met nor resolved. The result that survives is weaker:
$\lvert\nabla\phii_2\rvert$ is a \emph{rank} localizer (the true origin sits in
its top $\sim\!18\%$) that beats graph-centrality baselines (Perron, weighted
degree, deleted by measured correlation) and holds across media.

\subsection{Uncertainty quantification}
Morris elementary-effects screening (\code{results/e6\_metrics.json}) finds the
label most sensitive to diffusion $d_0$ ($\mu^\ast=500.7$), then fibrosis density
($147.8$) and transverse conductivity ($146.3$). A Gaussian-process surrogate has
leave-one-out $R^2=0.35$ (large residual anatomical/label variance beyond the
screened knobs, a label-noise caveat and not a positive). Across uncoupling
strengths $\Delta w\in\{0.18,0.36,0.54\}$ the grouped GBT
increment is $\dAUC=0.033$--$0.036$ with all $p\geq0.26$. This sweep runs on real
anatomy rather than the synthetic media: it uses the GM1 cohort, and the original
$62$-mesh Roney subset that predates the extension to all $100$. It therefore
speaks to the real-cohort null and not to the synthetic one. Every arm
sits below the pre-registered $0.05$ gate, but non-significance on its own does
not make these arms null, and because we report no interval for them they are
indeterminate rather than a demonstration that the null is robust to $\Delta w$.

\subsection{External inducibility-rate anchor (plausibility, not validation)}
Clinically observed AF inducibility spans $\sim\!5\%$ to $\sim\!85\%$ depending on
population, protocol aggressiveness, and the sustained-AF definition (Kumar et
al.\ 2012~\cite{kumar2012} report burst-sustained $14.8\%$ vs.\
decremental-sustained $41.2\%$ \emph{in the same patients}). Our labeller's Roney
$34.0\%$ ($34/100$) sits in the moderate-burst band (Marquardt et al.\
2018~\cite{marquardt2018} report $30.6\%$ in patients without prior AF), between
the no-AF-control floor ($5\%$, Oral et al.\ 2008~\cite{oral2008}) and the
AF/aggressive ceiling ($51\%$, Kawai et al.\ 2019~\cite{kawai2019}), consistent
with $\sim\!42\%$ post-ablation (Liu et al.\ 2020~\cite{liu2020}). The UW
$9.8\%$ ($8/82$ on the cap-corrected substrate that every predictive result
uses) sits just above the no-AF-control floor and far below the moderate-burst
band, for a cohort that \emph{is} AF patients; we flag that rate as anomalous,
and Section~\ref{sec:substrate} traces it to a difference in fibrosis burden
between the two releases rather than to biology or to the defects in the released
UW substrate. The Limitations document two substrate defects, and repairing both
moves the rate from $6/82$ to $14/82$, a difference that does not reach paired
significance (McNemar exact $p=0.077$, $n=82$) and that retains only two of the
six original positives; a direct control shows that the fibre defect is not
responsible for any of it. This is a base-rate plausibility check and not
clinical validation.

\subsection{Resolution convergence: the labeller does not converge in the tested window}
\label{sec:convergence}
The convergence study reported here is the project's single weakest point, and we
state that at the outset. On a completed six-tier study (Roney, $n=24$ at each of
1500/\allowbreak 3000/\allowbreak 6000/\allowbreak 12000/\allowbreak
24000/\allowbreak 48000 nodes; $144$ simulations,
\code{results/\allowbreak convergence\_summary\_roney.json}), the inducibility rate falls
sharply from the coarse mesh ($58.3\%$ at $1500$) to $16.7\%$ at $3000$, then appears
non-monotone across the fine tiers ($25.0\%$, $20.8\%$, $25.0\%$) and rises again
to $37.5\%$ at the finest affordable tier ($\sim\!50$k nodes), a
$17$-percentage-point spread over the last three tiers, so it has \emph{not}
settled.

At $n=24$ per tier the precision is low, which bounds how much of that pattern
can be treated as real. Exact binomial intervals are
$[36.6,77.9]$, $[4.7,37.4]$, $[9.8,46.7]$, $[7.1,42.2]$, $[9.8,46.7]$ and
$[18.8,59.4]$ percent across the six tiers. These are marginal intervals: the
same $24$ subjects are simulated at every tier, so each describes one tier's own
precision and discards the pairing, and overlap between two of them is not a
test of their difference. We use them only for the negative claim, namely that
every tier from $3000$ nodes upward overlaps every other, so the apparent wiggle
among the fine tiers is \emph{not} distinguishable from sampling noise and we do
not claim it is a real non-monotonicity.

The paired comparison the intervals cannot make is available, because the same
$24$ subjects run at every tier: for each adjacent pair the $2\times2$ table is
fixed by the two marginals and the agreement count, so an exact McNemar test
follows without any further simulation
(\code{results/\allowbreak convergence\allowbreak\_paired\allowbreak\_tests.json}).
That test separates the two halves of the pattern cleanly. The coarse-mesh drop
is real, with $11$ subjects losing their verdict between $1500$ and $3000$ nodes
against $1$ that gains one, $p=0.0063$, and it survives Holm correction across
the five adjacent comparisons ($p=0.032$); none of the four fine-tier
transitions is resolvable ($p=0.375$ to $1.000$ unadjusted, all $1.000$ after
correction). What the study establishes is therefore sharper than the intervals
alone allow and still sufficient for our purpose: the coarsest tier demonstrably
over-calls, while across the fine tiers the rate cannot be shown to have
converged and no tier can be declared the settled value. Distinguishing these
tiers would require several hundred subjects per tier, roughly two orders of
magnitude more simulation than the $144$ runs here.

The convergence finding limits the absolute numbers but not the comparative
claims. The rate does \emph{not} collapse to zero and the phenomenon is real;
what is resolution-sensitive up to $\sim\!50$k nodes is the magnitude. Because
the main results ran at $2{,}000$ nodes, every absolute number (inducibility
rate, competitor AUC, localizer origins) is coarse-mesh-provisional pending the
deferred openCARP validation. The two claims we lead with are untouched:
(a)~the relative SFI-vs-competitor comparison, since both arms score the
\emph{same} labels, so the shift in absolute AUC is common to them; and
(b)~the label-free estimator-validity argument ($\rhoval$ is computed from the
graph Laplacian, no labels at all). Sharing labels does not make the relative
comparison unbiased: per-subject label noise attenuates the incremental $\dAUC$
toward zero (Section~\ref{sec:labelnoise}), so that comparison is conservative.

%======================================================================
\section{Discussion}
%======================================================================
The four contributions are (1)~a pre-registered, leakage-controlled,
effect-size-gated evaluation of a plausible spectral biomarker, yielding a clean
null at synthetic scale ($10^5$ networks), where the mechanism remains inferred,
and a measured null on $182$ patients of real anatomy, where it is measured
directly and label-free (Section~\ref{sec:redundancy}); (2)~a label-free
validity criterion $\rhoval$ for the single-vector estimator, which proves
$\phii_2$ is ill-conditioned on real tissue ($\rhoval\approx2422\gg
\rhoval^\ast\approx3$) and supplies a portable a priori check for any
Fiedler-based marker, with the caveat that $\rhoval$ bounds estimator accuracy,
not predictive content; (3)~a weak-but-real, purely connectivity-derived rank
localizer that beats centrality baselines across three media but does not beat
substrate localization, the better localizer in the FHN arm
(Table~\ref{tab:localize}), leaving the strict pre-registered localization
endpoint null; and (4)~a demonstration that standard methodology manufactures
the exact false positives this protocol catches (leakage $+0.117$ AUC; the
$p$-value trap), with two worked instances from this project's own pipeline, one
in silico at $p=0.009$ and one on real patient outcomes at $p=0.0061$
(Section~\ref{sec:clinical}), the second caught by a mitigation fixed in writing
before the outcomes were joined.

\subsection{Three corrections, and what caught each}
\label{sec:corrections}

Three substantive claims in earlier versions of this work did not survive, and
because they are referred to individually where they arise, we set them out
together here. This is not the complete list: the project notebook holds the
full dated record, including further corrections that moved individual reported
numbers.

The first correction moved two numbers at once, since both rested on the same
cohort. An earlier version reported a combined real-cohort $\dAUC$ of $+0.051$
and read the primary endpoint as missed only on significance; separately, a
pre-specified fibrosis-heterogeneity comparison reported $\dAUC=+0.103$ at
$p=0.009$, which we read as evidence that SFI is more than re-encoded fibrosis.
Correcting the UW substrate at the original $144$ subjects took the first to
$+0.036$; extending the Roney arm from $62$ to all $100$ released meshes then
took it to $+0.012$ and the second to $+0.017$ ($p=0.452$). Both were caught by
more data, and for the primary endpoint the larger share of the drop came from
sample size rather than substrate. The withdrawn positive was vulnerable in the
first place because it rested largely on one cohort's $+0.340$ over a near-chance
baseline on six events.

The second correction concerns a causal attribution: we reported that the
degenerate fibre field in the UW release was the likely reason that cohort is so
rarely inducible. A direct control caught it, since destroying the fibre field on
a cohort that has an intact one left the inducibility rate at exactly $20/62$,
$p=1.0$. The explanation was plausible and mechanistically motivated, and it was
nonetheless wrong; no amount of further argument would have revealed that.

The third correction concerns a set of geometric figures. We stated that element
tag $164$ formed exactly five components, had exactly zero adjacency to fibrosis,
and left Euler characteristic $-3$ on removal; all three figures had in fact been
generalised from a single mesh or from a rounded mean. They were caught by
regression tests written over the whole cohort, two of which failed on first run.
The identification of tag $164$ itself survived and is better supported by the
census than by the original figures.

The three share a common feature: each was caught by a control, a larger sample,
or a test that this project designed and ran against its own results before
submission, and none was found by a reader. The first correction is also a worked
example of what the paper argues elsewhere, in that naive methodology (a $p<0.05$
threshold with no effect-size gate, no multiplicity accounting and no replication
on more data) would have reported $\dAUC=+0.103$ at $p=0.009$ as a finding. It
was produced by our own pipeline and it was false; the protocol that rejected it
is the same one applied to the primary endpoint throughout.

The validity screen itself is
domain-general. Spectral-graph fragility markers are used for seizure-focus
localization, brain connectomics, and power-grid vulnerability, and the same
precondition applies in each case: compute
$\rhoval=\lVert\Delta L\rVert/(\lam_3-\lam_2)$; if
$\rhoval\gg1$, the first-order spectral marker is outside its validity radius, so
its \emph{numerical} $\Delta\lam_2$ estimates and the stability of $\phii_2$
cannot be trusted. This is a statement about the estimator and not about whether
the feature ranks cases usefully, which must still be tested against competitors.

What this work does not claim should be equally clear. The mathematics is
classical, and no new theorem is offered.
$\rhoval$ does \emph{not} prove SFI is non-predictive, only that the
single-vector estimator is ill-conditioned; the predictive null is separate from
it, and is only partly redundancy (Section~\ref{sec:redundancy}). The real-cohort
result is a measured null in the sense that the interval excludes the
pre-registered gate, but it does \emph{not} exclude a small effect: the lower
bound is $-0.017$, so an increment of a few thousandths remains compatible with
the data and would need a far larger cohort to resolve. The localizer is
\emph{not} precise (strict endpoint null) and does not beat substrate imaging.
The $\rhoval$ scaling ``law'' is a clean match
to Weyl only on the real atrial 2-manifold. Labels throughout this section are a
monodomain simulator, not clinical outcomes and not openCARP; the one endpoint
scored against real outcomes is Section~\ref{sec:clinical}, which is not met and
whose apparent positive is an artefact.

%======================================================================
\section{Limitations}
%======================================================================
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item The labeller is strongly resolution-sensitive and does not converge in the
tested window (Section~\ref{sec:convergence}):
the rate drops from $58\%$ at $1500$ nodes to $17\%$ at $3000$ and rises to
$37.5\%$ at $\sim\!50$k nodes, above the
$34.0\%$ rate at the $2{,}000$-node operating resolution, so the direction of the
residual bias there is not established. At $n=24$ per tier the intervals overlap
for every tier from $3000$ nodes upward, so no ordering among the fine tiers is
resolvable and we do not claim that the non-monotonicity there is real; only
$7/24$ subjects are fully consistent across all six tiers. All absolute rates and
AUCs are coarse-mesh-provisional, while the relative comparison scores the same
labels in both arms, so the shift is common to them, though per-subject label
noise still attenuates the incremental $\dAUC$ toward zero.
\item The labels are simulator output rather than clinical outcomes: the
pre-registered openCARP ground truth was substituted with monodomain
Mitchell--Schaeffer, a deviation logged as such.
\item The clinical endpoint (Section~\ref{sec:clinical}) is underpowered by
construction, and its headline number is an artefact we report rather than a
result we defend. On the observed $48/82$ event
rate, power at the $\dAUC\geq0.05$ gate used for the simulator endpoint is
$0.24$ at best and $0.13$ under a weaker shared-noise assumption, so that gate
is \emph{not} reused there. The minimum detectable effect is $\dAUC\approx0.10$
to $0.15$, and $80\%$ power at $0.05$ would need $371$ to $859$ patients
(\code{results/\allowbreak power\allowbreak\_real\allowbreak\_outcomes.json}).
We therefore do \emph{not} report the clinical null as evidence that SFI adds
nothing to recurrence prediction; the study cannot resolve an effect of the size
that would matter. A further limitation is specific to this cohort and was
disclosed before the analysis: UW fibrosis is categorical rather than continuous,
which weakens the very baseline SFI must beat, and the baseline duly came out
below chance. A cleaner test of the clinical question would require a cohort with
continuous fibrosis imaging and several hundred patients, which this one is not.
\item Our first pass over the UW release was wrong in two ways, detailed with their
controls in Section~\ref{sec:substrate}: a fibre field that is a constant vector
in all $164$ meshes, which is a placeholder rather than data (fibre orientations
are IRB-withheld and meant to be regenerated), and element tag $164$, the caps
over the atrial orifices, which we had been treating as half-fibrotic tissue on
an undocumented release. Both readings have since been
corrected against the cohort authors' own tag legend. Repairing both
raises inducibility only from $6/82$ to $14/82$, and none of the three
substrate explanations for the cohort's low rate is supported by direct
control; Control~4 attributes the low rate instead to a fibrosis burden about
$25\%$ lower on the meshes it simulates
($0.248$ against Roney's $0.333$), reproduced by swapping the burden in either
direction. Its regions also rest on a PCA surrogate rather than
registered atrial coordinates. The cohort's predictive test is uninformative
about SFI, and what remains open is narrower: openCARP returns $4/82$ here and
the same rescaling does not recover it ($0/30$, Section~\ref{sec:opencarp}).
\item The inducibility label is stable in aggregate but noisy per subject, which
attenuates every $\dAUC$ we report. Destroying the Roney fibre field holds the rate
at $20/62$ yet flips $10/62$ individual verdicts, and repairing the UW substrate retains
only $2$ of its $6$ original positives. That compounds the mesh-convergence
instability above, where just $7/24$ subjects were consistent across six
resolutions. Non-differential label noise biases incremental AUC toward zero, so the
null we report is partly a statement about the labeller and not only about SFI.
\item The real cohorts involved $\sim\!30$ DeLong comparisons, and the
pre-registration fixes a multiplicity plan only for the clinical family of
\S8 (fixed-sequence gatekeeping, so that its primary is tested at the full
$\alpha=0.05$) and none for this simulator family, so every $p$-value we
report here is unadjusted; on the full $182$-subject corrected analysis
none reaches the pre-registered $\alpha=0.05$. The smallest is $p=0.121$
(\code{fibrosis\_vs\_+SFI}, gradient boosting, UW), and that comparison sits on a
base AUC of $0.562$ with eight positives, so it is uninformative rather than
borderline. An earlier version reported this family's combined estimate as the
study's one positive at $p=0.009$; it is withdrawn (Section~\ref{sec:corrections}).
\item $\rhoval^\ast$ and the random-geometric gap exponents are empirical; only
the real atrial 2-manifold cleanly matches the Weyl exponent.
\item \textbf{No sociodemographic characterisation of the clinical cohort is
possible from the released data.} Both cohorts are de-identified public deposits
of anatomy: they ship anatomical fields only --- a tissue or image-intensity
field, plus fibre directions and universal atrial coordinates in the Roney case
--- and no age, sex, race, ethnicity, comorbidity or socioeconomic variable
accompanies either release. Neither deposit contains an outcome label --- the two-year
recurrence outcomes were withheld from the public download and obtained by
direct request to the cohort's authors (Section~\ref{sec:clinical}). We
therefore cannot describe the clinical cohort demographically, cannot report
model performance within sociodemographic subgroups, and cannot assess whether
discrimination differs across them --- the fairness and health-inequality items
of the reporting guideline are unanswerable here rather than overlooked. This
also means the representativeness of these patients relative to any target
ablation population is unknown, and it is a limitation of the deposits rather
than of the analysis: obtaining those variables would require returning to the
originating centres under their own governance.
\item \textbf{There was no patient or public involvement.} This is a secondary
computational analysis of previously published, de-identified, publicly
deposited datasets, with no participant contact at any stage and no mechanism
through which patients or the public could have shaped the question, the
endpoint, or the reporting. We record the absence rather than leave it to
inference, and note that the endpoint definition itself --- documented
recurrence after a blanking period --- was set by the originating study, not by
us and not with patient input at this stage.
\end{enumerate}

%======================================================================

\section{Conclusion}
%======================================================================
A closed-form spectral fragility index does not add practically meaningful
reentry-inducibility prediction beyond existing features, either at synthetic
scale or on $182$ patients of real anatomy, where the combined estimate is
$+0.012$ with an interval that excludes the pre-registered gate. The mechanism
decomposes, and only half of it is redundancy: the aggregate SFI summaries are
reconstructible from the competitor set at $R^2=0.970$, while the extreme and
edge-level columns carry variance of their own that does not predict the label.
Independently of the predictive question, the single-vector estimator is
ill-conditioned on real tissue ($\rhoval\gg1$), and lawfully more so as meshes
are refined.

The same conclusion holds on real clinical outcomes, with a warning attached.
The pre-registered endpoint on two-year recurrence in the $82$ UW patients
returned $\dAUC=+0.197$ at $p=0.0061$, past both of its thresholds, and is
nevertheless not met: the baseline it improves on is anti-predictive at AUC
$0.250$, no feature on either side separates the outcome univariately, and nine
columns of random noise reproduce the increment. The analysis plan had committed
in writing to that reading before the outcomes were joined.

The core result is a carefully-scoped boundary rather than a discovery: where a
Fiedler-based estimator is numerically valid, why real tissue lies far outside
it, and how readily standard methodology would have reported a working
biomarker. It would have done so twice from this project's own pipeline: once in
silico, at $\dAUC=+0.103$ and $p=0.009$, withdrawn on more data; once on real
patients, at $\dAUC=+0.197$ and $p=0.0061$, caught by a guard written before the
data arrived. What the study cannot settle is the absolute inducibility rate,
which does not converge within the mesh resolutions we could afford; the
comparative claims, which score identical labels in both arms, do not depend on
it. Whether the null is an artefact of the stand-in labeller is addressed
directly rather than inferred from concordance: re-scoring the same comparison
against an independent solver's verdicts leaves it in place, with a combined
increment of $+0.028$ and no arm clearing the pre-registered threshold. That
re-scoring is an exploratory check, since the pre-registration bars those labels
from any endpoint until their calibration gate passes on both cohorts, which it
does not (Section~\ref{sec:opencarp}).

\section{Formal (proof-assistant) verification of the SFI algebraic core}
\label{app:formal}
%======================================================================
The algebraic core of the SFI edge-sensitivity identity is re-proved inside the
Coq 8.18 proof assistant and checked by its small trusted kernel, an assurance
level above a computer-algebra system. The primary file
(\code{formal/sfi\_edge\_identity\_Q.v}) works over the exact rationals
$\mathbb{Q}$, so \code{Print Assumptions} reports ``Closed under the global
context'' for every theorem: zero axioms. A mathematically identical
companion over the reals $\mathbb{R}$ (\code{formal/sfi\_edge\_identity.v})
inherits Coq's two standard real-number-library axioms and is kept only to show
that the statements hold over $\mathbb{R}$.

Combined with the classical Hellmann--Feynman theorem
$\partial\lam_2/\partial w_{ij}=\phii_2^{\!\top}(\partial L/\partial w_{ij})\phii_2$
